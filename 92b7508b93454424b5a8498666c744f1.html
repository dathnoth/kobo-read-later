The News

Anthropic's IPO details are now public

Anthropic has finally publicly filed for its IPO on the Nasdaq (this AFR story is much better, but paywalled), so we all get to check out the company's financial situation and how they plan to exponentially grow the fucktonne of investor money they've spent. They've valued themselves between US$1.5 to $2 trillion, making it the biggest IPO ever. Their S-1 form (aka prospectus) is available on the USA's SEC website and the first few pages are wild. They lead with AI "potentially" curing cancer, Alzheimer and heart disease, then go into how AI "raise productivity in every sector of the economy" and "raise the quality of government services". Wow, what can't AI do, I'd be crazy not to invest?! There's no exact date for when the IPO will take place, but I'm sure it will be all over the news, daily, until it happens.

Microsoft asks 60,000 employees to "voluntarily exit"

Microsoft is offering "voluntary exit offers" to a whopping 60,000 out of it's 223,000 person global workforce. An article in the Seattle Times says Microsoft is doing this to "create a flatter, more nimble company, with fewer layers of management and more accountability per employee, essentially blurring the line between employee and manager". Usually you only beg a quarter of your workforce to quit if something's gone very, very wrong in a business, but Microsoft's net income last quarter was US$31b and up 28% compared to the same quarter last year. Unless there's some sort of massive surprise in their upcoming full year earnings later this week, it looks like Microsoft's just doing this so Line Go Up, because the Line can never Go Up too much.

Apple updates the iPad mini

Apple quietly released a new iPad mini overnight. 8.5" OLED display, A20 Pro SoC, 5.4mm thin, an "Acoustic Display" where "Specially designed woofers are paired through the display to produce punchier bass and more volume and is Apple's first IP67 rated iPad. In addition, custom tweeters are now hidden between the glass and aluminum enclosure for clearer highs", uses the Apple N1 wi-fi/Bluetooth chip and C2 LTE modem. All that power in the SoC and it's only got 60Hz refresh rate? A faster Soc than the MacBooK Neo but I can't plug it into a monitor and use it as a Mac? Very disappointing. Anyways, pricing starts at $1,099, a large increase from the $799 the previous iPad mini launched at almost exactly 2 years ago, as is the unfortunately common trend of new Apple gear and indeed, all tech products in 2026. Apparently there's a slew of updated Apple products coming this week, all of which I assume will be unaffordable too.

Something I Saw On The Internet

NSW updates law so it's easier for apartment dwellers to install EV chargers

The NSW state government has passed changes to the Strata Schemes Management Act that give owners a "right to charge" for their electric vehicle, meaning that Strata Committees cannot "unreasonably object to the installation of the electric vehicle charging station". They also have an excellent website outlining the "five steps to EV readiness" you can point an owners corporation or strata committee to if they start giving you shit about burning the place down or who pays for the electricity, as well as templates for things like a survey of other residents to gauge their interest in EV charging, how to request EV chargers and basic info like what actually is an electric car and how they charge. Good stuff NSW, which to this Victorian's disappointment, is probably the best state in the country (their Metro is so damn good).
