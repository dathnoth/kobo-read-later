The News

Microsoft 365 was down worldwide for a few days

Large chunks of Microsoft 365 were offline globally most of Friday and well into the weekend before resuming normal service some time on Saturday night/Sunday morning. Outlook email being down for over a day was the main pain in the arse. I don't think I need to explain to readers of this newsletter why people and businesses being unable to access email for so long is a Bad Thing, but I did not expect an email outage to result in 15 people at a hotel getting chlorine gas poisoning because nobody could read the emails the monitoring system was sending. Email should not be involved in human safety systems!!

The only official statement Microsoft has given about the cause of the massive prolonged outage is telling the media: "This was a technical issue not related to cyber or AI / agents. The incident was introduced by an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced following a recent network change. We have addressed the underlying issue and continue to restore service to remaining customers and regions". It's 4PM on a Sunday in Redmond as I write this, so let's see what Microsoft says tomorrow.

The USA had to ground every flight for half a day due to a dodgy network router upgrade

Totally unrelated to Microsoft's issues, but at the same time, every single plane in the USA was grounded for about 5 hours on Saturday (Friday USA time), causing cascading flight delays and cancellations that are still causing problems for travelers now.

The FAA is still investigating the cause in detail, but as the Wall Street Journal explains: "Not long after L3Harris installed a new router made by HPE's Juniper Networks at the Arkansas location, the machine began sending out a high volume of data Friday afternoon. Data from that router and another at the Texas location overwhelmed the system, government and industry officials said. It led to 17 out of the FAA's 22 centers that oversee high-altitude flying to experience disruptions in communications, Bedford said, resulting in the FAA ordering the ground stop".

Bad timing as the FAA was recently proudly promoting how they're rolling out a new AI system called SMART (lol) to "predict traffic flows and identify potential conflicts before they occur". But the FAA boss said it isn't even "plugged in yet" so it wasn't the cause. Just a networking fuck up. This, plus Microsoft's oopsie, means its a good time to re-read Richard Cook's "How Complex Systems Fail". Still pertinent decades later.

Jensen Huang quits as CEO of Nvidia and a bunch of bootlickers want him to return

From Nvidia's website - "Jensen Huang has resigned as President, CEO, and a member of the Board, effective immediately. Colette Kress, current Executive Vice President and CFO of NVIDIA, has agreed to serve as Interim CEO". Bloomberg wrote up Huang's last hours before "resigning" and apparently this has broken him, looking "broken and shattered", his "usual take-charge demeanor had evaporated" and calling himself "a piece of shit". The commemorative plaque Denny's placed at the booth in one of their diners where Nvidia was founded, was removed, then re-installed.

A bunch of the worst people in the world - Donald Trump, Tim Sweeny, Elon Musk, Matt Mullenweg, David Heinemeier Hansson and more - reckon Huang leaving Nvidia is a mistake and that Nvidia is cooked without him, many comparing it to Apple getting rid of Steve Jobs, and many of them have signed a public letter to "bring Jensen back while an independent investigation establishes the facts" and "are concerned that Jensen was removed without meaningful due process".

The Wall Street Journal has a very enlightening article about Huang's replacement at Nvidia, Colette Kress, which outlines how internally she and Huang had strong concerns over how Nvidia was financing all these AI companies despite backing them in public. Even more enlightening was how worried Jensen was about an "avalanche" of GPUs and RAM coming into stock with nobody to take them off their hands. Some very noticeable bubble deflating noises going on right now.

SpaceX's Mechzilla catches a Starship upper stage

SpaceX used Mechzilla (a huge tower with mechanical arms) to do a "chopstick" catch of the upper stage of a Starship rocket for the first time over the weekend. You can watch a video of the impressive feat over on YouTube. The point of this is to then refurbish it, strap to some more rocket engines (which ideally were also captured and refurbished) then do it all over again. By capturing it with Mechzilla, it means Starship can be refurbished in the same place it takes off from, avoiding having to spend time, money and effort moving it to building, then back out again.

It is in stark contrast to ULA's Vulcan rocket that launched on Sunday and blew up after 10 minutes. Onboard were the first of 40 Amazon internet satellites. They were obviously destroyed, which sets back Amazon's plans to be a full Starlink competitor. It's also a setback for NBN, who plan to use Amazon's satellite internet service to replace the slow Skymuster satellites currently servicing the most remote parts of Australia.

Australian Institute of Criminology builds a profile of who is responsible for online hate

The Australian Institute of Criminology has published a study trying to answer the question of who the fuck are the people spending their lives on the internet spewing hateful crap in comments on social media? They did a survey of almost 10,000 Australian adults and found that 1.7% of those who responded admitted to subjecting others to online hate.

Compared to the rest of the people who didn't admit to being a prick online, "these respondents were more likely to be men, to be aged under 35 years and to be more impulsive. They were also more likely to have had previous negative life experiences (including victimisation to online hate), to hold prejudicial attitudes and to engage in a range of antisocial behaviours". They were also "significantly more" likely to have diagnosed mental health condition and have views like "superior groups should dominate inferior groups".

Full study is available on the AIC website.

Image Of The Day

NASA's Exploration Extravehicular Mobility Unit (xEMU) spacesuit undergoes antenna testing in NASA Johnson Space Center's anechoic chamber to inspect multi-layer insulation keep-out zones for the Wi-Fi and ultra-high-frequency antennas that are part of the spacesuit’s communication system. The xEMU test article is named xGUS, the successor to the Extravehicular Mobility Unit test article (also named GUS), which was named after NASA astronaut Gus Grissom and his iconic silver spacesuit. This image was taken from where the "horn," or source antenna, is located that sends out radio frequency signals to the spacesuit. The anechoic chamber walls are covered with a material that absorbs electromagnetic energy allowing the anechoic chamber to simulate a space environment. The antenna test facility is utilized to test antenna radiation distribution pattern performance for spaceflight applications in electromagnetic environments. Pictured in the photo is antenna test engineer Will Bond. (NASA)
