The News

Apple joins the folding phone party and other new Apple things

There's a folding iPhone now. It's call the iPhone Duo and it's an iPhone, that opens to a 7.6" screen, like the many other folding smartphones out there, but with the pains and joys of iOS. Pricing starts at $3,499 and will ship in October. The crease situation isn't as bad as other phones, but it's not creaseless. Will be interesting to see how the screen wears over a year or two of use. There's also an iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max with the usual faster SoC and better camera. The Apple Watch Series 12 and the Ultra 4 have two new unsettling features: Live Rewind, which "shows the previous 15 seconds of a conversation as a text snippet" and Siri Recap, that can "create high-level summaries of conversations that users can review later to jog their memory". I'm not a fan of normalising this type of passive surveillance. Maybe it's useful for some people, but at what cost for the rest of us? AirPods 5 are a thing if you need a new pair of earbuds.

Universal mobile service obligation passes House of Representatives & telcos aren't happy

Australia is getting closer to having a universal mobile service obligation, with a bill passing the lower house of Parliament this week. The law, if passed in the Senate, will mandate Optus, Telstra and TPG make sure "baseline mobile coverage, which includes – SMS and voice - is reasonably available outdoors to all people in Australia on an equitable basis" and that where there isn't coverage, they're "expected to use emerging direct to device (D2D) technology, provided over Low Earth Orbit Satellites (LEOSats)" - i.e: Starlink, Amazon Leo, etc. The telcos aren't happy, arguing that the "technologies needed to meet the obligation are too immature and won't be available as commercially competitive services in time for bill's commencement". They want the commencement date of December 2027 pushed forward to 2030.

Australia hasn't got enough EV chargers despite EV sales growing exponentially in 2026

Here Technologies and SBD Automotive have added Australia's electric vehicle infrastructure to their annual index for the first time and found that "Australia reached an electric vehicle-to-charger ratio of 24:1 in 2025", which is "considerably higher than typically expected" - i.e: there ain't enough chargers for the amount of EVs on the road. When I was on my EV charger startup bullshit a few years ago, the situation was dire at around 15:1 (most "developed" countries were in the 8:1, 12:1 range), so things are getting worse for public EV charging in Australia, not better. The 25:1 stat is for 2025 too, so things are likely crappier now as a shitload of EVs have been sold in 2026 thanks to high petrol prices. 25% of new cars sold in Australia during August were pure battery electric (for a total of 150,000+ sold in 2026 alone) and there's now 500,000 total on the road in Australia.

Matt Mullenweg put on leave against his will, hopefully the first step to the board firing him as CEO of Automattic

I don't know if anyone still cares, but "Matt Mullenweg, the founder and CEO of Automattic, best known as the parent company of WordPress.com, has been put on a leave of absence by his board against his will, according to a Slack message seen by TechCrunch". I won't go into the whole Mullenweg Automattic saga again (his Wikipedia page has a "public disputes" section with info on why this is happening), but old mate reckons the board has "conspired" behind his back to put him on leave without telling him. The CFO is now the interim CEO. Someone that spoke to TechCrunch "speculated that the timing may have something to do with Mullenweg’s annual trip to the Burning Man festival", which usually results in some dumb shit happening when he returns to work after attending.
