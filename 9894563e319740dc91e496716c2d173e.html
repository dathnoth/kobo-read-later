The News

Apple's John Ternus era begins today as Tim Cooks steps down as CEO

Tim Cook is no longer CEO of Apple, having officially handed over duties to John Ternus. Cook will remain at Apple as executive chairman. Tim's departing memo to staff is online if you're keen. Nothing particularly inspiring or interesting in it, old mate probably wrote it with the new Siri AI/LLM. While Apple means less to me these days as it did say, 20 or 15 years ago, it's still an important part of my life. I use their products multiple times a day and the Mac's origin story is still inspiring to me (btw the CHM put up a 6hr oral history with Andy Hertzfeld a few days ago, thanks for the heads up on that River). I'm cautiously optimistic that Ternus, a bloke with mechanical engineering qualifications, might not suck and might look back at Apple's history and use that as inspiration for what Apple should focus on next.

Big changes coming to Australia's Privacy Act

There's a fresh consultation paper outlining the government's intended changes to the Privacy Act. The headline change is a "right for individuals to request the erasure of personal information held by large digital platforms" - large digital platforms being "social media, email, messaging, streaming, gaming, and artificial intelligence platforms with a gross annual revenue of more than $500 million, or more than 2.5 million average monthly users". There's also "stronger standards for consenting to data collection, measures to stop businesses selling personal information 'without clear permission', and a 'fair and reasonable test' to limit companies data collection". If you'd like more info or to give the government your feedback on changes to the Privacy Act, check out the Attorney-General's website. They're hoping to get the updated legislation passed and in action within the next 12 months.

1Password donates $300k to vanity project of a guy who hates immigrants

1Password is "contributing $100,000 a year for the next three years" to the Omacom Foundation, the nonprofit entity that looks after Omarchy, a hot new Linux distro. A big company giving a little pocket change to an open source project is good, yeah? Not if it's the personal project of white nationalist David Heinemeier Hansson. It's bad enough that the project already has over US$12m for what is essentially a bunch of scripts to customise Arch Linux the way DHH likes it (imagine all the other OSS projects that could use a slice of $12m!), but to give that money to a foundation led by a guy that equates ethnic groups to wolves killing sheep, or that multiculturalism has failed and we need to deport all ethnics out of Europe, or gets upset when a chatbot won't translate his hateful blog post is not a good look IMHO! I wrote a blog post back in July looking at alternatives to 1Password.

Something I Saw On The Internet

Plexamp got its first update in a year and it's a good one

I thought it was abandoned after a year with no updates, but Plex pushed out a new version of Plexamp today! I no longer use Plex (I use Navidrome for music streaming now) but if you're still using Plex and having a local music library (or enjoy a mate's library), you'll love this. The update includes some very cool features, like being able to "calibrate the acoustic latency of your speakers" in multi-room mode by playing a "chirp" so all the speakers stay in sync, WASAPI output on Windows so the sound goes direct to your DAC unadulterated and IMHO the best feature, "macOS, Windows and Linux aren't running Electron anymore. Plexamp is now a native window driving the system's own web engine: much smaller download, a fraction of the memory, and native window handling throughout". Fewer Electron apps is a Good Thing.
