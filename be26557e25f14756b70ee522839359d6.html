The News

We might get a law making social media algorithms opt-in

Looks like the Australian government is considering making algorithms on social media opt-in, with the social services minister saying in an interview that "we're absolutely up for work on algorithms. Opting in to those recommender algorithms that are causing so much harm, opting in or opting out". Makes a lot of sense to me that this should be part of the much awaited digital duty of care bill the government actually had in the oven prior to the under-16 social media ban, but didn't get have a national radio station or advertising agency pushing it, so it's been simmering in the background despite every expert saying this is the law that might actually make a difference.

Debian says to contributors it's okay to use AI, but you're responsible for your code

Debian - my Linux distro for any servers I spin up - held a vote on the use of LLM/AI tools for things in/on/around Debian and the winning proposal was "responsible use of generative AI". You can read the full proposal yourself but in short, "Debian neither endorses nor prohibits the use of generative AI tools", but "expects that all contributions submitted to Debian, regardless of how and with which tools they were produced, satisfy the same standards of quality, correctness, maintainability, and legal compliance". Debian will "encourage our contributors to disclose whether a contribution was made with AI assistance, but do not require them to do so".

ARIA to ban songs from charts if there's too much AI in the track

ARIA has updated its charts code of practice so that "a recording developed using generative AI is eligible only where: it is substantially human made & it raises no stream or chart manipulation concerns". To determine if a song has too much AI in it, ARIA will rely on "definitions in the labelling standard announced by the global music community on 10 July". Those definitions appear to this in this press release from IFPI and it's just a voluntary label. If a scrupulous "musician" just doesn't apply that label and nobody can detect if it's AI or not, nothing happens? This is a big problem as highlighted in that IFPI press release - "AI-generated tracks comprised 44% of all new music delivered to its platform while Apple Music has said that more than one-third of the tracks uploaded to its platform are 100% AI".

Something I Saw On The Internet

Non-profits on cheap Microsoft 365 had data deleted then Microsoft rescinded offer

Here's a story about a bunch of non-profit organisations enjoying discounted rates for Microsoft 365 then one day, all their data was deleted, unrecoverable, when Microsoft changed the deal. Many of the organisations in the article reckon they got notice that one day, a change will happen, but no dates and certainly no warning that their data would be deleted. Just one day they woke up and their Microsoft 365 access was revoked and Microsoft just shrugged their shoulders when asked "can I get my data back at least?". Hard to know if Microsoft gave lots of warning and these folks are too busy/not tech savvy enough to notice, but it's total bullshit from Microsoft to not at least keep the account in read-only mode and allow data to be exported in a big lump file.
