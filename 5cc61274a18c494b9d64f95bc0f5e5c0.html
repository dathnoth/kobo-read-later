The News

eSafety Commissioner has common sense thoughts about smart glasses

The eSafety Commissioner put up a thing on their website a few days ago, outlining their position on smart/pervert glasses. Their position seems to be that smart glasses need "safety by design", which could take the form of "clear visual indicators when recording audio or video", "automatic blurring of people's faces", "disabling recording when smart glasses are not being worn", "delays and moderation for livestreaming" and "users should not be able to use smart glasses to obtain information about members of the public". That consultation paper on Privacy Act reforms I mentioned yesterday mentions smart glasses a few times so maybe some of eSafety's ideas will find their way into the Privacy Act one day, but what do we do in the 12-18 months until that happens?

USA sues Amazon for fiddling with sponsored product placement auction market

A new big tech court battle is kicking off in the USA, with Amazon sued by the Federal Trade Commission and 22 states over misconduct in the advertising auction platform it operates for sellers to bid on stuff like "Sponsored Product ads, Sponsored Brands ads and Display Ads alongside the results that appear when a consumer searches for a product using a keyword on Amazon's store". The FTC reckons Amazon's juicing of the auction market using what are "essentially shill bids", "generated tens of billions of dollars of revenue for the company". I'm not really shocked Amazon did this, it's not that different to what Google did and I'm sure Facebook does with their advertising platforms, but I am surprised that Trump's FTC is doing something about it.

Neal Stephenson to judge GPU World short story contest with $100k of prize money

Paradigm (a "technology investment firm") and Guardian Angel Intelligence (a "digital twin LLM" I think) managed to convince legendary sci-fi author Neal Stephenson to be a judge in their $100,000 short-story contest, GPU World. They want 1000-5000 words imagining "what will our world be like when (not if) every human being has access to the equivalent of a Fable or Sol LLM 24/7/365?". It's cringe, but I'm interested in reading a few stories based on this premise. Ironically, the competition says that "LLM use is permitted, but discouraged; we remind participants that LLM use tends to reduce originality and writing quality, and the flaws are especially obvious when LLM outputs are read as a group---unskillful use of LLMs will reduce the odds of being the best entry. We request disclosure of AI use".

Something I Saw On The Internet

Dyson's $800 toothbrush uses AI to shoot water between your teeth while you brush

Dyson is selling an $800 electric CameraJet toothbrush with a built-in camera. It uses that camera to do "Gap Optical Targeting" to shoot water between your teeth so not only does it brush at the same time, it flosses too. The "1mm intraoral camera analyses 28 images a second, processed by Dyson's AI machine-learning algorithm, to accurately target interdental gaps". They even have "scientifically engineered formulations" (aka toothpaste), "which allow the camera to accurately identify and target gaps", as well as proprietary brush heads. Hilariously, you can watch the camera live on your phone while you brush. I fucking hate flossing so if this thing actually works as described I would buy one, but knowing Dyson's past history of overselling and under delivering, I feel like it's ability to replace flossing is unlikely. I'd rather buy Breville's AI toaster.
