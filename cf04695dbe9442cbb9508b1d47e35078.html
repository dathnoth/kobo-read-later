The News

Apple software update day

Apple dumped iOS 27, macOS27 and all the other 27s on us today. Six Colors reckons iOS 27 is "an OS that feels like a marked improvement over its predecessor, in everything from design to performance to capabilities", with "a ton of small stuff — those quality of life improvements that chip away at little frustrations and annoyances" and "in general, iOS 27 feels peppy". Here's a nice 40 min overview video of iOS and iPadOS 27 if you want to go deeper and Mitty started a thread on the Sizzle forum to discuss the update. macOS Golden Gate is similar - lots of small tweaks and Siri as the only "big" feature, which honestly, I couldn't give a fuck about as I do not trust LLMs to do things properly. Six Colors sums up Golden Gate as "a very good update that rectifies macOS 26 Tahoe's greatest sins". There are updates to watchOS, visionOS, iPadOS, audioOS (HomePods!) and tvOS, but I don't care enough about those to write about them, but if you care, you should click the links like people did without hesitation 20 years ago to learn more.

Plug-in solar to be legal in Australia by mid-2027

At a press conference on the weekend, federal energy minister Chris Bowen said that the government is gonna make plug-in solar and batteries a thing in Australia, setting a deadline for their regulatory approval mid-2027. If you're unfamiliar, these are solar panel and battery kits that don't require an electrician to install. You hang some panels outside, plug them into an inverter and sometimes a battery, then plug the battery or inverter into a power socket in your house so all the stuff in your house can tap into the battery and panels. It's very popular in Germany and starting to take off in the UK. Here's a YouTube video reviewing the EcoFlow Stream kit. This press release from Solar Citizens, one of the lobby groups tirelessly pushing this, has some more detail on how they see it happening in Australia. There's some work to do with updating Australian electrical standards to support plug-in solar, but unusually, everyone agrees it is a good idea and there doesn't seem to be any vested interests spreading FUD to get in the way, so maybe we can have nice things for once.

Steam Frame VR headset reviews are in and the verdict is: pretty good

Embargoes on reviews of Valve's $1,609 Steam Frame VR headset that can either play games with or without a PC, have been lifted, with reviews from The Verge, Tom's Hardware and Rock Paper Shotgun having graced my eyeballs this afternoon. Some money quotes from those reviews: the "Frame's so comfortable that I don't mind wearing it, and it feels like one of the most exciting betas I've ever tried. Like the Steam Deck at its debut, this is gadget nerd wish fulfillment. It's a chance to watch Valve, one of the most intriguing companies in gaming, build a plane while flying it" - and - "Unreliable wireless connections: fixed. Tricky setups: simplified. Weird controllers: normalised. Needing a Meta account: not needing a Meta account. When the Frame can do this much, this well, I'm practically forced at inside-out sensorpoint to conclude that if I was buying a VR kit for myself, it'd be this one". I would also like to buy one, but I have too many games and not enough time as is, so will pass.

Something I Saw On The Internet

Web banners were annoying in the 90s, now I wish they'd return

Banner Depot 2000 is an "interactive archive containing 22,915 web ad banners that existed on Chinese- and English-language web pages in the late 1990s and early 2000s. The banners are extracted from archived web page snapshots of 77,747 URLs featured in six printed Internet directory books published between 1999 and 2001 in the United States and China". This was peak internet advertising IMHO. If websites just had the occasional banner or two, vetted by the owner of the website, paid for at a rate that made both publisher and advertiser happy, linking back to someone else's website - the WWW might still be a sustainable place for publishing written word stuff instead of the zero sum game it turned out to be where Google "won" and ruined the web publishing ecosystem with it.
