The News

Patch your Plex server pronto

If you haven't updated your remotely accessible Plex setup lately, stop reading this and do it now as there's a vulnerability in older versions that is currently being exploited. Plex2Shell chains multiple exploits together to give whoever points it at an IP address, remote code execution capabilities. Jerks read this and have been installing cryptominers and residential proxies on any publicly accessible, outdated Plex install they can find. Plex fixed this stuff a while ago, but if you're like me and only update your self-hosted stuff every now and then instead of automatically, then chances are you're waving a red flag to the internet with "use my computer to mine cryptocurrency!" written on it. Probably a bad idea to run Plex wide open to the internet in the first place (pop it behind a reverse proxy at least, a VPN even better), but lots of people do it!

13GW of renewable energy projects planned for Whyalla

South Australia announced a whopping 13GW of proposed renewable energy generation in Whyalla, with Windlab, Altra, Heron and Neoen given permission to apply for a licence to operate solar, wind and battery on a huge chunk of Crown land. There's not a lot of detail on the specific projects as all the SA government is really announcing is these companies now have "the exclusive right to apply for Renewable Feasibility Licences and undertake the detailed technical, commercial and environmental studies needed to determine whether their projects can proceed" and will still need to apply for a Renewable Energy Infrastructure Licence, before they can start building. However, 13GW of new renewables is huge. To put that into perspective, as I write this at around 2PM, the entire QLD/NSW/VIC/SA/TAS (aka, the NEM) demand is around 14.7GW. Obviously it'll be rare (possibly never) to get all 13GW pumping into the NEM at once, but that's still a massive set of projects. Good luck to everyone involved.

Something I Saw On The Internet

Watch out for thin, counterfeit power cables

he evergreen Gough Lui writes about an IEC C13 power cable (aka kettle cord) that started to melt after only 3 minutes of use and how that led him to build a rig to put a bunch of IEC cables he owns through a load test. Eight cables he owns did not meet the 10A rating printed on the cord! The wires inside the cable are supposed to be a certain thickness so they handle a constant 10A load without melting, but in some cables, they're way thinner than the markings printed on the outside of the cable. So yeah, counterfeit IEC cables are now a thing to worry about, cool, I love that for us as a society. Can't even trust a damn cable.
