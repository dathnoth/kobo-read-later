The News

X wins in federal court against eSafety Commissioner

X sued the eSafety commissioner last year, claiming that it shouldn't be designated under Australia's Online Safety Act as both a "social media service" and a "relevant electronic service". Yesterday, the federal court agreed with X. eSafety tried to argue that the Online Safety Act "does not preclude a service from falling within more than one category of digital service", but the judge disagreed saying that interpretation "would not sit comfortably with the Act's structure" and would allow eSafety "to create a standard and apply it to a provider who is already the subject of another industry sector code and effectively bypass the consultative industry-led model". Will be interesting if eSafety decides to appeal this, because this ruling means other social media services that eSafety claims are also "relevant electronic services, now only have to pay attention to the less onerous social media code.

Google has new Pixel phones, watch and more

Google has some new phones and other Pixel branded accessories. The Pixel 11, 11 Pro, 11 Pro XL and 11 Pro Fold are a thing. Faster, better, more expensive and so on. One of the main features appears to be HiLight, "an LED indicator light for things like Gemini interactions and incoming calls from favorite contacts". You know smartphones are becoming a commodity when an LED notification light is a feature worth pointing out. There's a new Pixel Watch that can do "blood pressure trends" and "insulin resistance trends" - note they aren't measurements, but "trends". An impressive feature coming to Pixel phones soon is an update to Live Transcribe that uses a new AI model to convert American Sign Language into text. I would have thought that anyone deaf could also type, but not always according to Google.

Bevan Slattery reckons latest cable cut off the cost of WA is suspicious

Earlier this week, CEO of Subco (also founder NextDC, Superloop and more) Bevan Slattery posted on LinkedIn that there were "shunt faults on two systems (Indigo West and Indigo Central) straight after each other and in close proximity to each other. Remarkably this also happened in the Submarine Cable Protection Zone off Perth Australia. Coincidently some suspicious/coincidental activity from a vessel near the location and timing of the shunts occurring", going to say that he wants the AFP to investigate what went on with the damage to this submarine cable, insinuating that it wasn't just a mere accident, but maybe something deliberate. Tom's Hardware followed up with the AFP, who said they received "a report of crime in relation to this matter" and are looking into it. If you go to the comments on the LinkedIn post, there's an interesting back and forth between Bevan and a geologist/mapping guy, who claims the cable is in a shit spot extremely prone to this sort of thing.
