The News

Telstra to deploy iOS and Android RCS messaging capability in December

Telstra dropped this little nugget of info on their website late last week: "Rollout of RCS will begin for Android customers from 16th November and iOS customers will begin to be able to use RCS after December's iOS release. Rollout of RCS features are scheduled for completion by the end of the year" - yep, cross-platform RCS in Australia at last! The linked article explains why RCS is nice to have, but the big benefit will be a less shit experience for group chats containing a mix of iMessage and Android users. No word if TPG or Optus are rolling out RCS yet, or if Telstra MVNOs (e.g: Boost, Belong, Aldi etc) will get access to RCS too.

NBN has begun over-provisioning upload speeds by 15% at no extra cost

Also in "what, a telco doing something useful for us plebs at no extra cost???", NBN started over-provisioning upload speeds over the weekend. They've done this on download speeds since 2020, but now upload speeds get the same love. That means everyone on FTTP, HFC, fixed wireless and FTTC will get a 15% speed boost to their uploads and the poor souls on FTTN get around 4%. My 500/50 connection now chugs along happily at 540-545 down and 53-55 up. Nice! Leaptel explains that over-provisioning "gives the provider some headroom to account for network and protocol overheads, making it easier for you to consistently achieve the advertised speed of your plan". Thanks NBN.

Universal Outdoor Mobile Obligation passed both houses of Parliament last week

The Telecommunications Legislation Amendment (Universal Outdoor Mobile Obligation) Bill 2026 was passed on Friday arvo. Not a lot of media write-ups about this one, but the comms minister's press release says it "will require national mobile carriers Telstra, Optus and TPG to provide access to SMS and voice coverage for Australians, almost everywhere you can see the sky", "made possible by a combination of existing land-based towers and new Low Earth Orbit Satellite technology, which allows for mobile signals to connect Direct to Device". Telcos have until the 1st of December 2027 to make it happen, which seems very optimistic to me, and probably means all the telcos will have to do a deal with Starlink as it's the only viabale LEO satellite system right now, but this is a good thing overall IMHO.

Something I Saw On The Internet

Ads suck, but they suck even harder now that "zero click targeted spyware" ads exist

The Citizen Lab published a report last week that explains how they discovered "zero-click spyware delivered via targeted ads" on Al Jazeera's website, spread by a mob called Candiru/Integrity Labs via ads purchased by "Discover-Tech, an Israeli company that presents itself as a legitimate advertising company, but which we found operates as a facilitator for multiple mercenary spyware partners", who operates "a platform called Controlium that enables its mercenary partners to de-anonymize, fingerprint, and automatically infect devices the moment an advertisement is rendered - a form of zero-click attack". Pretty wild stuff and a great example of how fucked the online advertising industry is.
