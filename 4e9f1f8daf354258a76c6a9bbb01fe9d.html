The News

QLD premier confirms Anthropic's gonna build some huge datacentres hooked up to gas power

Anthropic has done a deal with Zerra DC to be part of the $31.9bn Western Downs Digital Park about 37km from Dalby in Queensland. Neither Anthropic or Zerra has said anything about it, but the QLD premier is doing a victory lap saying that the deal "allows our state to be at the forefront of locking in data sovereignty and adding energy generation for lower power prices for Queenslanders and employment opportunities". No doubt a big attraction for Anthropic is the huge Darling Downs and Braemar gas power plants that are right next to the "digital park", and Queensland's rock hard enthusiasm for fossil fuels. It's unclear exactly how much electricity the joint will use, but Zerra DC's project page reckons Western Downs Digital Park will have a "540MVA substation" and will be 100% air-cooled.

Government thinking about what to do with TV broadcast infrastructure while viewing audience shrinks rapidly

On the 70th anniversary of TV broadcasts in Australia, the communications minister put up a discussion paper to get feedback on the future of Australia's free to air television infrastructure. Less people are watching terrestrial TV every year, but the infrastructure used to broadcast it is a fixed cost regardless of how many people tune in. There's going to be a point in the near future where the ad revenue from free to air TV is going to be lower than what it costs make those terrestrial broadcasts happen. It's already happening in regional parts of Australia. The paper outlines three scenarios that are mostly around reducing the amount of spectrum required by using more efficient compression and reducing the amount of channels, expanding the satellite TV system (VAST), and/or upgrading to DVB-T2.

NBN's CEO is probably the highest paid public servant in the world

If you had to guess who the highest paid public servant in Australia is, who would it be? The CEO of Australia Post? The Prime Minister? Nah, it's the boss of NBN Co, Ellie Sweeney. She was paid $3.43m last year, with her base salary bumped from $1.49m to $2.5m and her bonus more than doubled to $406,00 to $891,025. The linked article reckons she might be the highest-paid public servant in the world (if you don't count bribes or leveraging insider knowledge). Not only is NBN Co's CEO paid well for a public servant, she's paid well for a CEO full stop, exceeding the average pay of ASX200 CEOs. Apparently the justification for the high pay is that executive roles for these types of government businesses are benchmarked against similar ASX companies to achieve "market competitive remuneration" and "lure talent from the private sector".

Something I Saw On The Internet

A comparison of iPhone Pro cameras and info about Apple Reference Images

I've seen many people say the only reason they buy a new iPhone is to get better camera quality, so this handy article from DPReview comparing the cameras on the iPhone 18 Pro all the way back to the iPhone 12 Pro could be of interest. Unsurprisingly, the older the phone you've got, the more likely you are to get a substantial improvement in camera quality. If you've got an iPhone 16 Pro for example, you will likely not notice a difference in the main and wide cameras at all, but on the iPhone 15 Pro, the main and wide lenses on the 18 Pro are a decent step up. On the topic of iPhone cameras, the iPhone 18 Pro added a feature called Apple Reference Image, that give photographers a "trustworthy, scalable guarantee that a reference image is what it claims to be: a real photograph, captured by a real sensor in an iPhone camera, at a specific time". Interesting stuff.
