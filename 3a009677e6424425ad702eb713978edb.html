The News

iPhone Duo's display and hinge are a little fragile

It appears that Apple's iPhone Duo may not only be expensive, but also quite fragile, as there's more than a handful of reports of the display "audibly cracking" not long after people unbox their fancy new phone. iFixit did their usual tear down on the Duo and came up with a theory as to why screens are cracking - "In our closing simulation below, the center support bar fails to drop far enough. The screen's curved fold comes down onto it, creating a small stress hotspot near the reported break location". There's some pretty fancy analysis going on here, the entire article is worth a read. Folding phones have always been delicate and looks like Apple's is no different, with JerryRigEverything finding it a bit less durable than other folding phones. I still want one though.

Trillion dollar asset manager isn't convinced AI will take off outside of tech

Apollo Global Management have about a trillion dollars of stuff they manage and are one of the world's largest private equity firms. Rich people give them money to make them even richer, so when Apollo's chief economist writes: "Despite massive investments and widespread hype around artificial intelligence, the technology has delivered zero financial payoff to corporate America outside of tech" and that "there is no evidence that revenue growth or margins are improving outside the tech sector", along with a bunch of charts in a blog post, I'm gonna remember it when Anthropic and OpenAI and SpaceX try to suck off our politicians into doing things against Australia's best interest so they can cling to their turgid valuations.

Print edition of Australian Personal Computer to cease after 46 years

RIP to Australia's longest running computer magazine and the longest surviving English language PC magazine - Australian Personal Computer, aka APC. It has been published every month since May 1980 but will soon cease being printed, along with Future's other print publications Australian Hi-Fi, Sound + Image, Australian Camera and ProPhoto. Kinda surprised APC survived this long and is probably due to editor Ben Mansill, who I had the pleasure of working with for a few years at PC & Tech Authority and is one of the best people I've met in this industry. I love print magazines and think they'll have a renaissance one day as the WWW collapses and people grow desperate for something more substantial than a 90 second video clip.

Something I Saw On The Internet

Australia has the cheapest electricity in the world

An article in the AFR based on analysis by Rystad Energy has found that Australia, and Victoria in particular, has the cheapest wholesale electricity prices of any major economy in the world. I can't find the full report to dig deeper, but I believe it due to the high amount of residential solar and batteries hooked up to the grid - a thing most other countries simply do not have. Electricity generation is only a third of our bills, there's transmission and distribution costs, plus the retailer's cut on top of all that, but it still surprised me to read that our electricity is cheap. The downside however, is that because it's so cheap, it makes the businesses cases for more of it weaker. That's a big problem as coal plants close and their generation needs to be replaced by something else.
