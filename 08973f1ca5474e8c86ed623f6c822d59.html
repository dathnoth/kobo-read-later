The News

Apple HomeView smart display is a thing, new Apple TV 4K & HomePod mini 2

Apple's got a new device to sell ya - the HomeView. It's a 7-inch display plopped on a speaker that feels like mashup of other Apple products. It's got "Faces" like the Watch, is a touch screen rectangle like the iPad mini, but there's no App Store on it, relying on projection (like CarPlay) from your phone instead. The HomeView can act as hub for HomeKit and Apple has partnered with LG and Schneider to design and sell more HomeKit stuff under a "Home Collection" branding. If you've squeezed your life to fit in Apple's neat little box, this $449 unit is for you. The 3-min promo video for it is... interesting. Do people really live this way? Apple also very mildly upgraded the Apple TV while jacking up the price (also $449!) and announced the HomePod mini 2 that can be used in a 4-speaker wireless surround setup with the Apple TV which is kinda cool. And while we are in the Apple news pit of despair, they've launched a campaign trying to convince people that Safari is better than Chrome (battery life, performance, privacy - the usual).

Australia has no budget to replace its ageing supercomputers

The Pawsey Supercomputing Research Centre hosts Australia's fastest computer - Setonix. It was built in 2022 with a life expectancy of 4 years and somehow, the government hasn't committed to funding Pawsey to the level where they can replace Setonix with something bigger and better in 2028. Unfortunately, due to the cost of computer bits these days, Pawsey CEO Mark Stickells reckons building a new supercomputer will cost "four to five times" as much as the $70m spent on Setonix. If the government doesn't cough up the cash, Australia will be left without a supercomputer for various research projects. Demand for Setonix and Gadi (the nation's other big beefy computer, operated by the NCI) is "approximately three times available capacity", so it's not like nobody's using it. It would be incredibly short sighted for the government not to just plonk a cheeky half billion on this and give the nerds doing valuable research work a reason to get excited. At the very least it's a cool photo-op for Albo and his mates.

We're getting a National EV Charging Roadmap & NRMA is hiding why it's struggling to build one

ARENA gave the Electric Vehicle Council a tidy $1.06m in funding to develop a National EV Charging Roadmap. This plan will "bring together car manufacturers, charge point operators, consumer groups, electricity networks, fleet operators and many more to develop the evidence base of future charging needs that will help plan Australia’s charging system for what comes next". As I've written before, public EV charging isn't catching up with the exponential growth in EV sales so maybe this report will help, even though the answers are very obvious to me (just install more of the fucking things??). Meanwhile, NRMA's "Grit in the Gears" report that explains why they only delivered 62 of the 117 promised EV chargers across Australia with $39m of ARENA funding, is still sitting in FoI purgatory despite a Liberal Senator requesting the the energy minister tabling it in Parliament (which would mean it's made public) and the deadline for that to happen flying past weeks ago.

Something I Saw On The Internet

X is a shithole, stop going there, don't even look at it, it doesn't deserve us

Two of who I'd consider quite influential tech writers - Casey Newton and John Gruber - have gone crawling back to X. Both of them make the argument that despite Elon Musk being a fuckwit, X is where all the juice is for the tech (really, the AI industry) is now, so they have to be there. Karl Bode wrote a great post about how selling out your principles while at the top of your game is bad actually. Read the whole thing, but here's a little excerpt I liked: "the most bullish AI boosters don't want to talk about politics. They don't want to think about authoritarian corruption. They want to play with their toys. They're quite often predominantly interested in money. When they wander into spaces where the tech industry's abysmal ethics are prominently disputed, they get squeamish and conclude the problem is somehow everybody else". Amen brother. Tim Bray also wrote a good post explaining that X still sucks, despite people like Gruber and Newton saying it's not so bad now.
