Nvidia's CEO admits having an affair with an alleged Chinese spy

Nvidia's board of directors posted a short message to their website this morning that they're "initiating an independent investigation in light of recent reporting that founder and CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort". A few hours later, CEO Jensen Huang posted his own message to the Nvidia website that begins with, "I owe my family and all of you an apology. I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry".

This all came about after a New York Times report detailing US government investigations into how Chinese AI companies keep getting their hands on GPUs that Nvidia is supposed to be banned from exporting to them. They learned that a Singaporean company called Megaspeed and its CEO Alice Huang (not related!) was allegedly recruited by the Chinese to build a relationship with Nvidia's CEO and spy on him, presumably to aid Chinese interests due to Jensen's closeness to Trump.

It's unclear what information the spy was able to get out of Jensen but if they were having an affair you gotta assume they were pretty close. It's also unclear if the information the spy was able to get out of Jensen made any material difference to China's AI efforts. This news only sucks if you're a paranoid American government that considers China having a lead on AI a bad thing. It also sucks if you're Jensen Huang, who might be forced out of the company he founded, at the height of its wealth, because not only is he greedy in business, but in his sex life too.

Europe has another crack at trying to tame TikTok's algorithm

After an "in-depth" investigation, the European Commission found that TikTok breached the Digital Services Act (DSA) with its shitty algorithm. From the press release: "TikTok did not adequately assess how its recommendation algorithm may lead users into such ‘rabbit holes', putting their wellbeing at risk" and that "repeated exposure to such content may have serious negative effects on users' wellbeing, including increased feelings of sadness or anxiety, body dissatisfaction, lower self-esteem, or worsening of existing eating disorders and mental conditions".

The EC reckons while TikTok did some mitigation of the "rabbit hole effect", they don't work - for example, "using the 'not interested' button may not be enough to change the content feed. The algorithm prioritises showing users content based on viewing time, over their choice to see less of certain content". Pretty obvious to anyone that's self-aware and used TikTok, but it's nice to see a large institution like the European Commission realise it too.

They want TikTok to change the For You feed's design to "rely more on users' explicit preferences, disable viewing time as an indicator to recommend content, increase the accessibility and usability of user controls, and regularly test its algorithm". Good luck with that Europe, I wish you the very best on this endeavour as the TikTok algorithm, in my opinion, is pure evil and everyone involved in its creation should be in prison.

NSW to end BYOD programs in primary schools next year

The NSW state government is ending bring your own device (BYOD) requirements for public primary schools next year and schools will now supply devices for kids to use. The press release announcing the move pitches it as parents no longer being "required to buy expensive laptops or tablets because of a school BYOD policy", which always struck me as weird for primary school kids. Secondary schools will still have BYOD, but "students cannot be required to bring their own devices and that alternatives must be available".

It also plays into that whole federal government Digital Detox thing I mentioned last week. The press release mentions some research about how "screens can be a major source of distraction for students, with 84% of high school students and 57% of primary school students reporting they were distracted when using screens in class" and that the OECD reckons "Australian students are nearly twice as likely to spend more than 2 hours per day using digital devices for learning as the OECD average, while academic results are falling".

The RBA's chief economist lays out their view of where AI is at and where AI might be going

Sarah Hunter, the RBA's chief economist, gave a speech at the UBS Australasia Conference this morning and the full thing, plus charts, is posted on the RBA's website. If you're interested in how AI will impact the Australian economy, and therefore, our lives, I strongly suggest giving it a read. Some choice excerpts:

"It is difficult today to know what the new jobs and industries will be, or what overall effects AI will have on the economy. Current estimates for the impact of AI on annual productivity growth over the next decade range from over 2 per cent per annum to just 0.1 per cent" - i.e: it could be fuck all, or could be a game changer.

On current evidence that AI has failed to materially lift productivity: "This experience mirrors that of previous general-purpose technologies. Their implementation typically led to what economists call a productivity J-curve. That is, there is an initial period of weak or falling measured productivity as firms invest time and resources into experimenting and undertaking the necessary complementary investment and organisational changes to take advantage of the technology, before productivity rises as the technology becomes embedded".

AI's impact on inflation: "The effect is likely to vary over time. In the near term, the positive shock to aggregate demand set against the economy's current supply capacity will put some upward pressure on inflation", but "over time, supply will catch up with demand as data centre capacity and related sectors expand, easing current bottlenecks. If AI delivers broader productivity gains, economies will be able to produce more with the same inputs. As a result, the initial inflationary pressures from AI investment are likely to dissipate".

On jobs: "There is little evidence that AI has had large effects on aggregate employment in advanced economies, although there are emerging signs of weaker hiring, particularly among younger workers in highly AI-exposed occupations" and "contacts in the RBA's liaison program indicate that AI has not yet impacted most firms' headcount and is not expected to over the next year".

Interesting stuff IMHO, puts the AI hype into perspective and without the usual pro-AI/anti-AI bias some economists can exhibit.

The big and beautiful new Powerhouse Museum in Paramatta opens this weekend

The all new, $915m Powerhouse Museum in Parramatta NSW, opens this weekend and The Guardian has a sneak preview along with a few dozen pics of the fitout. The opening exhibit is Task Eternal, "a celebration of flight and space travel, it takes over the museum's main hall", that is "overwhelming in its scope and visual magnificence".

There's also "a tribute to the social, cultural and design history of shopping centres" and The Dark, "the Powerhouse's exhibition for children" that is "set in a massive replica meteorite more than 22m long" where photos aren't allowed and smartphones have to be put into a locked bag before entering. Check out the website, there's heaps going on over the next month to celebrate the opening.

The photos look phenomenal and I've already planned a trip to Sydney just to check it out. I love museums of all kinds and it's so rare to have a government spend almost a billion dollars on anything related to STEM, let alone a museum!

Something I Saw On The Internet

Aussie infosec researcher finds a way to send remote commands to any of a certain car maker's vehicles

TRS80 shared this blog post in The Sizzle's Slack group. It's heavy on technical info, aimed at an infosec audience, but this paragraph at the end gets to the point: "Let's take a step back and think about what we achieved here. We could send commands to any of the automaker's vehicles in Australia, across both of their brands, by only knowing the number plate of the vehicle. The final chain looked like this: Plate -> VIN -> Subscriber GUID -> OpenIDM admin credentials in JS vendor bundle -> Path traversal to bypass the WAF -> MFA bypass via ROPC flow -> Minting a consumer app token -> Remote command API". Yeah, that's bad. There's a video of the exploit in action that shows them unlocking the car. The post doesn't disclose which car maker it is, but the top post on Hacker News reckons it's Toyota, going by the cars in the video.

Interested in adding soil moisture data to your Home Assistant setup? This post is for you

SmartHomeScene got 10 smart soil moisture sensors and did a nice comparison of how they work and their compatibility with Home Assistant. You'd use these types of sensors to know when to water your plants, as some plants like moisture and some don't. Super handy device and I have one of the cheap AliExpress units in a pot plant that seems to work fine. SmartHomeScene liked the Ecowitt WH51 best, and it is IP66 rated so can be used outside, but needs a gateway/dongle to talk to Home Assistant. Surprisingly, the cheap Tuya Zigbee sensors are very good too, but you can't use them outdoors.
