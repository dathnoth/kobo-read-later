The News

Privacy Commissioner and Attorney-General also aren't fans of smart glasses

Federal Privacy Commissioner Carly Kind published a blog post musing on smart glasses. I agree with her that "there is a meaningful difference between place-based surveillance, such as that we find in airports and in certain retail spaces which have been able to meet the relevant thresholds to justify its deployment, and surveillance wearables in the hands of every roving individual, designed for discretion (or even concealment)". It seems like the Attorney-General agrees too, saying in a media release that they want the Privacy Commissioner to give this issue "priority consideration" because smart glasses "have significant privacy implications, especially for women and children who could be disproportionately affected by inappropriate recording, harassment, surveillance or other harmful conduct enabled by this technology". There's also a petition on the federal Parliament's website requesting "an immediate ban on the sale, importation and promotion of AI-enabled smart glasses with recording or facial-recognition capability".

NT government having a sook about feds telling them 100% gas powered data centres are verboten

Part of the federal government's attempt to appease community anger about massive data centre proposals popping up around the country is to force them to at least use renewable energy to power themselves. This has upset the NT government, who are all-in on making the Territory an AI hub, with a $40b project fuelled by all that filthy gas in the Beetaloo basin their flagship project. It's debatable how strict the federal mandate for renewables will be - Ketan Joshi makes the claim data centres are greenwashing their use of renewables, and even the federal energy minister reckons gas will still be part of "sensible firming arrangements" - but if anywhere in the world would be suitable for gigawatts of data centres powered by 100% renewable energy, it's gotta be the NT, right?! How can extracting, refining and transporting gas be so substantially cheaper than getting a shitload of solar panels and batteries from China over the data centre's useful life?

Dawn of the Machine is a new Quake expansion pack to celebrate 30 years of this landmark game

Quake is 30 years old and to celebrate this milestone, id Software have made a "brutal new episode", Dawn of the Machine, that's free for existing owners of Quake. The "expansive new episode features 19 all-new maps across a cohesive campaign, alongside a brand-new soundtrack, hidden secrets, a dedicated episode hub, and an all-new deathmatch map". I know what I'm doing this weekend!! My Dad told me a story a few years ago, how the IT dude at work gave him a bunch of floppy disks one day and said, "give this to your son, he'll love this game". It was a demo of Quake and he was right! That guy was such a legend, giving me his old computer magazines, books and random software all throughout the 90s. Looking back, he is a big reason why I got into computers. I don't think it was on purpose, just one nerd being nice to a junior nerd, but it meant so much to me at the time. I wish I could find him and thank him.

This $400 keyboard is tempting me like a fresh apple pie cooling on a windowsill tempts a 50s cartoon character

Elaborate custom mechanical keyboards have never tickled my fancy. I admire them and the people that get to express themselves via their keebs, but the $10 Dell/HP/Lenovo pieces of junk are just fine for me - but I am very tempted to spend $350-$400 on the Altar II, an extremely thin, mechanical keyboard that looks cool as hell. It's like Teenage Engineering decided to get into making keyboards designed for Mac users. I'm trying so hard to save money now that The Sizzle is my only gig and the Lord drops this on me. I must be strong.
