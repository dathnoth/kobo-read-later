The News

Digital Duty of Care bill introduced to Parliament

The Digital Duty of Care bill slid into Parliament yesterday. The Conversation has a good overview of what it does and what the changes are from the draft. The big change is that the Communications Minister no longer has the power to determine what is or isn't "harmful content" and any changes to the types of stuff too naughty for Australians to see will be done via Parliament passing amendments to the law. Because the government doesn't have a majority in the Senate, it needs help to pass the bill and the Greens want a right for individuals to sue tech companies and bigger penalties put in, whereas the Coalition is calling it a "dog's breakfast", "censorship dressed up as online safety" and don't want the bill at all, so expect a few more changes later in the year as a Senate inquiry does its thing.

House House boycotts Microsoft/Xbox with Big Walk

Big Walk is a fun and popular game made by the Melbourne-based House House but despite its popularity, Big Walk is not on Xbox, was never promoted as available for Xbox and likely won't be coming to Xbox because House House are boycotting Microsoft due to their co-operation with Israeli war crimes. If you need a reminder about Microsoft's involvement with the genocide of Palestine, The Guardian explains how the dystopian tracking of Palestinians by Israel is done on Microsoft Azure, with the full endorsement of Satya Nadella and how Azure is the platform of choice for the IDF's combat and intelligence needs. Good on House House for being on the right side of history despite the financial and reputational hit they'll take for it.

Tesla Roadster does a party trick, still no ETA on when it'll go on sale

Tesla redesigned the Roadster first announced a decade ago and held a livestream showing off its wild capabilities, using rockets to jump over a lineup of Tesla's other vehicles and do 0-60mph in 0.8 seconds. The car has thrusters all around it to make it do stuff in the air, along with a fan system for extreme downforce when it's on the ground, that gives it the ability to "fly" (if you're invited to buy the model with the thrusters) and achieve speeds of up to 612km/h according to Elon. Tesla is taking deposits for it in the US, but there's no final price nor is there an ETA when it'll actually arrive in TikTok influencers garages. It's basically a halo car for the fanboys to aspire to and the ultra-rich to show off to their mates. Anyways, as someone on Hacker News said - "Good on Elon for putting out a car that embodies his best trait, sucking as hard as possible".

Image Of The Day

The Xerox 914 was the first commercially successful automatic office copier. Using Chester Carlson's xerography process, documents were produced electrostatically, using powdered toner. This copier weighed 650 pounds and made one copy every 26 seconds on paper up to 9 x 14 inches. The 914 also came with a "scorch eliminator" - a small fire extinguisher for taming fires caused by overheating. (The Henry Ford)
