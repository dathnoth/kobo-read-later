The News

Sydney man charged with hacking after asking ChatGPT to download PDFs off court website

A bloke in Sydney was charged last year with four counts of "accessing restricted data held in a computer", after asking ChatGPT to scrape the NSW Department of Communities and Justice's Online Registry, which included over 9,000 "sensitive court documents". He pleaded not guilty in court this week, arguing that he wasn't hacking the registry, but "attempting to help a friend who was undergoing bankruptcy proceedings". There's not a lot of detail in the news articles, but it appears that the Online Registry was poorly (perhaps even not secured at all) and ChatGPT made a Python script to download these "sensitive" documents. The guy that prompted ChatGPT doesn't seem to be an IT professional and was just following ChatGPT's instructions to get what he wanted off the website. Seems to me like yeah, there's a bit of FAFO for blindly doing what ChatGPT said, but also the NSW justice department severely over reacting.

Anthropic reports dozens of examples of people and organisations "misusing" Claude

Anthropic's Detecting and countering misuse of AI: September 2026 report is a collection of malicious activity on Claude that Anthropic noticed in the last few months. Those activities include "cyber operations, influence operations, surveillance, scams and fraud, biological misuse, conventional weapons development, and distillation". Biological misuse is an interesting one, with Anthropic detecting a few instances where Claude was being used to develop grant applications for modifying viruses in a way that would make them suitable for biological warfare, or a pandemic. Some other activities Anthropic shut down includes "a Yemen-based guided weapons engineering cell using Claude to develop guidance software", "a China-based surveillance and recruitment operation targeting Uyghurs in Syria" and even "an automated pro-Awami League fake-news operation on Claude targeting rural Bangladesh". Ahh, the democratisation of technology, you love to see it.

UNSW to give every student and staff member access to ChatGPT & will encourage use

UNSW is rolling out ChatGPT Edu and Codex to 80,000 students and staff after testing it with 10,000 staff over the last 12 months. Apparently they're first uni in Australia to give everyone access to an LLM (I guess Copilot with Microsoft 365 that practically every uni in Australia has access to doesn't count?). According to the vice-chancellor, "Our ambition is to produce AI-capable graduates" and according to a 2nd year masters of IT student, "AI is here to stay. The technology is only getting better, so it's about learning how to use it effectively rather than rejecting it altogether". Don't worry about AI being used by students to cheat, UNSW has "a strong commitment to academic integrity and transparency" and has developed a framework to "helps educators clearly communicate expectations about when students may use AI, when its use is prohibited, and when students are expected to use AI". Okay Mr. Fox, I'll let you into my chicken farm, but promise you'll use our framework when deciding which chickens to eat.
