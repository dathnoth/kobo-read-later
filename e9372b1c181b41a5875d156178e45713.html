The News

Ledger hardware cryptocurrency wallet supply chain attack

Ledger - a manufacturer of hardware cryptocurrency wallets sold as a more secure alternative to keeping your wallet on a computer or smartphone or exchange - confirmed that an unknown number of Ledger Nano Xs sold via a Malaysian-based reseller were compromised and that it has multiple confirmed reports of funds being drained from the devices remotely. Mark Karpeles (former CEO of Mt. Gox - remember them?!) posted pics of the compromised devices, showing how they've been modified with an LTE modem and antenna. Professional looking stuff too. Dunno how many Ledger Nano X devices are out there in this compromised state, but you've got to assume all of them are fucked and Ledger's reputation is cooked. Props to whoever did this though, pretty cool hack if you ignore the whole theft being bad thing.

Epic planning to make a Linux version of Fortnite

Epic Games put up a blog post saying they "vibe coded a new Epic Games Launcher that's way faster and more responsive than the current one, and has a new look", with some charts showing how it launches faster than Steam - was this a thing people complained about?? They're also rebuilding Easy Anti-Cheat for Linux (which already exists but apparently sucks?), "working with distro maintainers on security requirements", which once complete, will enable them to bring Fortnite to Linux. This blog post has more info on what they have planned for anti-cheat software on Linux and why it's a dealbreaker for bringing the massively popular game to Linux. I wonder why they're bothering? Is Linux gaining that much traction in the gamer space (thanks Steam!) that they feel they need Fortnite there? That's awesome if so.

Please give the Open Australia Foundation money

Ever used the site They Vote for You to look up how much of a grub your local federal MP is? What about Right to Know, that super handy site that makes it easy not only to make a freedom of information request but to look at what the government supplied? Or maybe you've poked around with Planning Alerts to get a heads up on what property developers are cooking up for your part of the country? Those are all run by the Open Australia Foundation and they're running out of cash. Donations have dropped but costs to operate these services remain, so give them a few bucks a month. Even if you and I may not use it regularly (or ever) what the Open Australia Foundation does benefits all, and the country would be worse off without it, so I started giving them $5/m as a thank you for existing.
