The News

USA is upset about Australia's Digital Duty of Care legislation

Australia's US Embassy has made an "extraordinary public intervention" as the ABC described it, on the proposed Digital Duty of Care legislation, publishing their thoughts about the law on the embassy website. They say they've got concerns about "restrictions on speech", "prescriptive product design mandates" and "expanded litigation risks that unduly burden American companies". The embassy didn't seem to give a shit when the Online Safety Act was being cooked up, or when the ban on kids under 16 was floated, but the Digital Duty of Care seems to have them really pissed off. That's how you know it's probably going to actually do something (which experts in this field have been telling the government for years), unlike the previous attempts. I hope we don't cave in and lick the US boot like we usually do.

Anthropic's got a new frontier model and OpenAI has a 50% cheaper model

The two big boys of frontier AI released new models within an hour of each other (what a coincidence) with fancy blog posts - Claude Opus 5.5, which Anthropic says is the "strongest-performing model we've tested to date" and GPT-6 Sol & Luna, models based on the bigger GPT-6 Astra that "help distribute the benefits of that intelligence by advancing the frontier on cost efficiency". The big features of these models appear to be that they aren't so expensive (though Opus is still pretty damn expensive), probably due to intense pressure from Chinese models that are like, 90% as good for half the price. I don't know how aggregate benchmarks like the ones Artificial Analysis conduct, translate into real world use, but Opus 5.5 is now the leader on its Intelligence Index if that means anything to you.

FBI hacked, personal info on employees now being held ransom

ShinyHunters popped the FBI's careers portal - lol. The hacking group told 404 Media that they "hold data on all FBI employees and applicants" and provided a sample of the data that contains "an alleged address, phone number, date of birth, and in some cases details on their spouse", which 404 Media was able to confirm. ShinyHunters said they "used a zero day exploit in an Oracle product called PeopleSoft", which got them access to AWS GovCloud. When asked if ShinyHunters will do their usual extortion scam on the FBI, they said "what we plan to do is not something I’d call extortion, maybe coercion" and that it is not "financially motivated", referring to "false allegations" the FBI made about the group in a report. If the FBI can't even be arsed to keep the personal details of their employees secure, what hope is there for the rest of the industry with much less on the line than the FBI?

Something I Saw On The Internet

PrivTracker is a private torrent tracker designed for personal file sharing

Need to share big files with a bunch of people? Do those people know how to use BitTorrent? Check out PrivTracker - "unlike public trackers, it shares peers only within a group using the same announce URL. It really works like a private tracker, but can be generated with one click of a button", "using UPnP, most torrent clients can automatically handle port opening, and only one person in the group needs an open port for everyone to download. Unlike public trackers, PrivTracker shares peers' IPs only within the group and keeps files off public networks like DHT, ensuring privacy and efficient sharing". Nifty.
