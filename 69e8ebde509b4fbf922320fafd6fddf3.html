The News

Everyone's patching PHP servers today and the normies are concerned

Looks like that PHP vulnerability I mentioned about yesterday has broken containment and non-tech people are starting to pay attention. There's an article about PHP on the ABC (hi Cam!), the NSW government put out a press release saying they are "aware of and responding to security concerns about the PHP scripting language", some Australian businesses are taking sites offline until they can get someone to patch them, and even the Japanese prime minister posted on X urging us all to patch ASAP or take the server down if you can't patch it. The PHP Foundation made a post explaining the vulnerabilities and how it was leaked prior to the patch made available, and there's a website showing the exploits in action. I patched The Sizzle's server (I use a bunch of shitty PHP scripts I hacked together for all the admin/backend stuff) this morning!

auDA comes to its senses regarding .com.au ownership rules

Good news for .com.au domain owners (like me) - auDA has changed its mind and will open a new round of public consultation on its proposed changes to who can own a .com.au domain due to the very strong reaction when their advisory panel released its "final report" a few weeks ago. If you forgot, auDA wanted to limit .com.au registrations so a business can only register and own a .com.au that matches its ASIC registered business name and would deregister domain names businesses currently own unless they're an exact match for the business name or a trademark. The Sizzle would have lost thesizzle.com.au under those new rules, but the plan now is for it to only apply to new .com.au registrations. Still a bit lame, but way more sensible.

Aussie class action lawsuit against Meta for harming kids

Law firm MinterEllison has kicked off a class action lawsuit against Meta in the Federal Court, "on behalf of children and teenagers who have suffered harm as a result of addictive design features and exposure to harmful content on Facebook and Instagram while they were under the age of 18". It's the first time Meta's been subject to a class action lawsuit in Australia. You can sign up to be part of it on the class action's website. Meanwhile, Meta copped a US$25b fine in the US state of New Mexico for the bullshit it pulled during the Cambridge Analytica scandal. The judge said "a penalty of more than $219 billion was warranted based on state law", but reduced it to $25b. Meta is gonna appeal, so who knows what they're actually pay.

Something I Saw On The Internet

iPhone Duo review embargoes lifted, is a classic 1st-gen Apple product

Reviews of the iPhone Duo are out ahead of its release tomorrow. The Verge reckons the Duo is "expensive, it's full of compromises, and it can be incredibly frustrating to use", but like it because it's "fun, in the way the best early adopter devices are fun". Wired said that "the nanotexture finish on the inner screen is the star of the show. The matte surface reduces the crease's visibility, while also minimizing glare and reflections. It also feels like e-paper". 9to5Mac has a bunch of screenshots of popular apps running in the various screen modes, thinks "switching from Face ID to Touch ID has been the hardest thing to get used to on iPhone Duo" and that " there are aspects of the software experience that need to catch up to the hardware". Many reviews reckon the iPhone Duo "runs hot" to the point of being uncomfortable to hold at times. I want that first-gen day-one Apple product dopamine hit, but they're bloody expensive and they're sold out until Feb 2027, so I'll just pretend the iPhone Duo doesn't exist.
