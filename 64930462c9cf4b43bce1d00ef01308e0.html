The News

SSL certs are cooked

Indian certificate authority eMudhra's private keys have been compromised. This is very bad because eMudhra was trusted by all the major browser vendors, which meant everyone else trusted them too, so any device or app trying to verify if a certificate is legit would see it's from eMudhra and go "yep this is a legit CA", when in-fact, it's so easy to pretend to be eMudhra, it makes doing a man-in-the-middle attack relatively trivial. Most modern devices have already received updates, but anything older or unsupported likely won't. This website has a little test you can run to see if the device you're using still trusts eMudhra. If you have anything that connects to the internet, you better update it ASAP.

PHP is cooked

Also in "computers were a mistake" areas, PHP2Shell is a fresh exploit tool that gives remote code execution ability to anyone that runs the script and points it at a PHP server of any version, as far back as PHP 5. The vulnerabilities the tool takes advantage of were supposed to be disclosed at the same time as PHP released patches, but the vulns got leaked by accident and it didn't take long for someone to cook up a tool using them to cause mischief. There's often juicy databases, or API keys to other services on PHP servers, so if someone can easily gain shell access to that server, huge amounts of shitfuckery can occur. PHP will release a fix at around 1AM tomorrow, but CERT-EU is recommending that "organisations should consider isolating internet-facing applications from public networks until a fix can be applied" - that's how quickly they expect exploits appearing in the wild and how severe the exploit is. Fun!

NextDC is cooked

NextDC's share price shit the bed by 40% today as it appears the market has woken up to all the data centre builder shenanigans post-Firmus failing to achieve an IPO. In NextDC's case, they've revised their projected net revenue down by a third, blaming issues getting power to their West Footscray data centre where the local community freaked out at the sight of 66kV transmission lines in their street and it remains unknown if the local council will allow those upgrades so M3 can go from ~60MW to the 225MW as planned. Meanwhile, Senator David Pocock spoke at the National Press Club and proposed a 5% levy on Australian data centre revenue that he claims would raise $500m by the end of 2030, giving the example of how Microsoft's data centre business generated $2.3b in local revenue, but paid no company tax. I recommend giving the full speech a watch.

Wayback Machine is cooked

Brewster Kahle has a piece in The Atlantic begging everyone to stop blocking the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine as we all scramble to block rude and aggressive LLM/AI bots from scraping our sites. He writes: "blocking a nonprofit library is the wrong response to this problem. Impeding the Wayback Machine's preservation work will not prevent powerful AI companies from gathering training data. It will certainly not restore the advertising-based business model that supported newspapers in the 20th century, a business model that has long since collapsed. What it will do is prevent historians, researchers, courts, ordinary readers—and, most ironic, journalists themselves—from consulting an accurate record of what once appeared on the web". After reading Brewster's plea, I was reminded of dolphins stuck in those fishing nets designed for our insatiable desire for tuna.
