The News

SIM Card Sharing Coming To iPhones

While the dirtiest Apple laundry is being aired in court and everyone between Mexico and Canada is really pissed about Apple blessing the President's lake renaming (don't worry, in Australia we still see the OG name), I'm personally more excited about huge launch of stuff coming down the line over the next week or so. One of those things is new operating systems, and one I had not read about yet was iPhone Handoff. It requires carrier support and an eSIM (I emailed all the Aussie telcos about it and got no replies), but you'll be able to have two iPhones sharing the same SIM card. Maybe the new iPhone fold is just two iPhone Airs gaffa-taped together so they need to be able to share SIM cards?

Packard Bell Is Back, Baby!

There's a chance that me telling you how warm, fuzzy, nostalgic, and young I feel about the words "Packard Bell" will age me, seeing as though their 486SX mid-90s model rented from Radio Rentals was my everything as a young teen, but there's the greater chance that it will bring us all together as well, and if that's so, please take each other’s hands as we sing Kumbaya together and look at the Acer website to see that they have relaunched the 100-year-old brand (linking to a non-existent packardbell.com) with the Packard Bell DotBook, which will be price-equitable to the MacBook Neo and the new Galaxy Book6, but we don't have Australian availability yet. And if you love the name DotBook, there's no current mention of a DotBook Pro or Air; instead, we've got the DotLoop, DotTune, DotLine, DotLook, DotShine, DotComb coming down the line, though I'm personally all about that DotBass. Isn't capitalism beautiful? Tip of the hat to Acer also for conceptualising what it would look like if the DotBook and a Nokia 9210 had a child.

Something I Saw On The Internet

Want To Buy A Really Expensive Apple Computer?

If you thought $28,699 AUD for a new fully spec'd Apple Mac Studio (M5 Max 36-core CPU, 80-core GPU, 256GB RAM, 16TB HD) was a lot, then add a zero and double it so you can put a bid down on the Hatfield, an original Apple-1 motherboard. We're talking a whopping 8KB RAM and an original Apple cassette interface board. Signed by Woz along its top edge and packaged with a typed letter signed by one Steven Jobs, at the time, Vice President, Operations of Apple Computer Inc., to the computer's original owner, Fred Hatfield, authorising the exchange of the Apple-1 for an Apple II 4k board which would have really done wonders for Fred's local AI performance.
