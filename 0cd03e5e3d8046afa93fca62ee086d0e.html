Google was going to force Gemini on schools that only wanted Search

Google said they're going to disable Search for any K-12 Google Workspace for Education accounts if the admin of those accounts (i.e: schools) don't also opt-in to Gemini, Gemini Notebook and Gemini in Classroom products. Google's effectively saying unless you give your students access to Gemini/AI, we won't even let you use Google Search. After everyone cracked the shits, Google told the media they'll "allow schools to enable the Search service alone, without turning on access to additional Gemini products", but "schools would still not be able to use Search without certain AI features like AI-generated summaries, given AI's growing integration into the company's search functionality". There should really be a way to disable those summaries, because as Kagi said in an opportunistic blog post, "The educators asking for choice aren't anti-technology. They have spent years in the classroom, and they know firsthand that handing kids AI-generated answers doesn't build critical thinking; it weakens it".

AMD expects to make more money than Intel for the first time ever

AMD reckons its Q4 2026 revenue will exceed Intel's for the first time ever - a bit of a moment for those of us old enough to remember AMD as the scrappy little upstart against Intel who dominated the PC industry. AMD plans to earn US$17b next quarter, mostly off the back of EPYC CPUs and Instinct AI accelerators (aka GPUs with huge amounts of fast RAM without video output) whereas Intel predicts US$15-16b due to "weakening PC demand and manufacturing capacity constraints" - i.e: Intel just hasn't got a compelling AI GPU offering. Unfortunately, it means that AMD's Q3 2026 revenue from "client and gaming" (aka Ryzen & Radeon) dropped 40% from the year before, predominately because the cost of RAM makes building a PC or buying a game console (Xbox, PlayStation, Steam Deck use AMD CPUs & GPUs) too expensive, so people just aren't buying new ones, and AMD expects the PC market to "decline by a double-digit percentage" next year.

More detail on how Windows 11 will be more friendly to PCs with 8GB of RAM

Microsoft's published a blog post explaining how they're gonna tweak Windows 11 to make it suck less on PCs with 8GB of RAM. Better memory compression, "briefly prioritizing the work you ask for and backing off background tasks such as search indexing while you're active", enabling "Efficiency Mode by default" in Teams, inactive tabs in Edge "go to sleep after 15 minutes by default, down from two hours" and cutting the amount RAM things like Widgets and Office apps use. Overall, "on 8 GB PCs, Windows is now up to 20% more responsive, and multitasking is up to 40% faster in our lab test". Why wasn't this stuff done regardless of the AI-induced RAM shortage???
