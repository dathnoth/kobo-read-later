The News

We can all play GTA6 now

GTA6 is out!!! You can go to the Xbox Store or the PlayStation store on your respective consoles, slap down $130 for the standard edition or $160 for the ultimate edition (adds a bunch of different looking cars, clothes, weapons etc) and play it right now. NBN said it hit a traffic record last night (36.54 terabits per second) with people pre-loading the 200GB+ game. If you want some technical details on the game's resolution (it's 1440p upscale to 4K, locked at 30fps), then WCCF Tech has some quotes from Rockstar about it.

A few lucky bastards got to play GTA6 early so they can write about it. Here's reviews from The Age, The Verge, Bloomberg, The Guardian, and Anandtech. The good news is that they all like the game - it's currently sitting on a score of 99/100 on Metacritic. David Swan over at The Age said it's a "heady mix of Goodfellas, Heat, Tiger King, True Romance and Pulp Fiction" and that "in an era of AI slop, this is a game that feels deeply alive and human, which is fascinating given how many people it took to make and how long it took". The Verge put in their review headline that GTA6 "lives up to the hype". Bloomberg says it is a "masterpiece". Big claims that as soon as I finish today's Sizzle, I will be able to verify for myself.

Anthropic to IPO tomorrow as it's competitors release new frontier & cheaper models

As Anthropic is set to go live on the Nasdaq tomorrow, Microsoft, Google and OpenAI have all released new models that claim to either be superior to Anthropic's Fable or Opus, or almost as good but way cheaper.

OpenAI's GPT‑6.2 Astra "is the best model in the world for scientists" and "come close to or outperform other leading models at a small fraction of the cost per task". Gemini 4 Neon is fresh today, currently available via the API and ranks highly on Artificial Analysis, but at a third of the price of Fable and Astra. Microsoft is also taking the "we're just as good as the other Anthropic and OpenAI, but way cheaper!" approach, announcing "a family of seven new world class models at a truly exceptional price-performance". MAI Code 2 apparently "reaches 98% of the coding quality of GPT-6.1 Sol at about 40% of the price. And against Opus 5.5, it reaches 96% performance for just 5% of the price".

It's really tough to compare all these models. What a benchmark shows versus the output you get in the real world are totally different and unreliable. I genuinely do not know how people decide which models to use for which task?

Microsoft explains how an old bug and poor testing cause massive email outage

Microsoft graced us with an explanation for their large outage last week. Unfortunately it is via X, not on a public, official Microsoft website:

"First, a latent defect caused servers to become overwhelmed and unresponsive under certain load conditions", "Second, a surge in load increased the likelihood for the networking gateways to encounter the contention" and "Third, at the same time, nearly 14% of server capacity for the networking gateway was out of service due to an unrelated issue from the previous day". "Taken together, the increased load and the reduced server capacity, caused the network gateway to encounter the latent bug from July. This caused requests to build up until servers ultimately stopped responding. This occurred during the peak window of traffic in the United States".

The Verge's Tom Warren has a good article based on info from his Microsoft contacts, explaining the mess.

Bunnings and Firaxis told a lot of nerds to find a new job

Bunnings has given the arse to 230 IT workers and 120 IT contractors after doing a deal with Tata Consultancy Services to outsource their jobs to India and the Philippines, starting in December. A Bunnings executive said that "we need a technology model and partner that keeps pace with our customers so that we can continue to deliver the service, value and helpful advice people expect from us", and that they are in a "daily sprint to keep up with what our customers want and need, now and into the future" - which means sacking people?? Anyway, Bunnings plans to "scale" its use of AI and automation to "reduce manual tasks and improve efficiency". Their operating earnings (aka profit) went up 5% year over year.

Also in company I have given a lot of money to over the years does a bad thing - Firaxis, the mob responsible for the Civilization franchise of games - sacked a third of its staff, about 60 people. They said they're "restructuring and recentering around the studio's core strength — designing world class strategy games", so the "size of the studio is being reduced to focus on design, technology and the player experience, which means saying goodbye to talented colleagues". Hope this doesn't result in the planned expansions to Civ 7 (i.e: Earthrise, that looks really cool IMHO) taking longer than planned!

Coles & Woolworths got hacked, aren't giving any details

Late on Monday afternoon, Coles published an "update" on their website to say they're "currently investigating a cyber security incident". So far they "have not identified any evidence that customer, team or supplier data has been compromised or removed from Coles’ systems", but Coles is working with "global cyber security experts to contain and secure the Coles IT environment", as well as the Australian Cyber Security Centre. So, uhh, what happened? If no data was compromised or removed. What did they detect? Just someone logging in and doing nothing? What was even the point of publishing this post? Something fishy is going on here, but Coles is being super vague about it.

Meanwhile, there's dozens of reports of Woolworths digital gift cards getting drained via unauthorised transactions. Unlike other gift card scams where someone accidentally buys tampered physical gift cards, these are digital ones, sold by Woolworths themselves and Woolworths redeems them into your Everyday Rewards wallet immediately. Woolworths isn't explaining themselves, but did say "we have commenced issuing replacement gift cards to ensure customers impacted are fully reimbursed and are working to complete this as quickly as possible" - as someone that uses discounted Woolworths gift cards often, I'd love to know how this could happen in the first place. Going by the ABC article, I don't think it's poor security practices by the gift card purchaser.

Something I Saw On The Internet

People are pondering how and why we should begin to abandon the internet

A bunch of smart people were asked the questions - What would have to break, or be built, before people would be willing to leave today's internet behind? Who would lead such an effort, and who might stand in its way? What from today's internet and the platforms that define it is worth saving, and what should be left behind? What would a better, healthier, more sustainable and pro-democratic network look like, and how would we build it? - their answers were published on this Tech Policy website. Asking these questions would have seemed incredibly naive or even stupid say, 15 years ago, or even a decade ago, but now, Dead Internet Theory and all that, kinda seeems like a good series of questions to ask.

Fmail 3 is a nice macOS web wrapper for Fastmail & Penguin Mail is a neat mail client for Linux

Do you, like me, enjoy and use Fastmail for your email and calendar and stuff like that? Are you a Mac user? Then you might enjoy FMail 3. It's basically a web wrapper around the quite nice Fastmail web interface, but with little hooks to make it better integrated into your Mac. Personally, I just use the built-in Mail and Calendar clients on macOS/iOS, but FMail 3 looks cool. Oh, if you're a Linux user, you might like Penguin Mail. I use it on my Linux laptop as a native mail and calendar app (also with Fastmail - but it claims support for Microsoft and Gmail) and it's really nice. I honestly prefer it to Apple's Mail and Calendar.

Getting a usable image out of a digital camera sensor is more difficult than I thought

This blog post explaining how an image of a Christmas tree is generated after light hits the sensor on a digital camera is fascinating. It starts off as a grey blob, then a black and white blob with some white dots, then the dots are coloured, then some mucking around with RGB channels, denoising, white-balancing and more work with colour curves. It's crazy how much work is done just to get a "basic" image out of a digital sensor. It makes any "post-processing" I might do to change brightness or sharpness trivial compared to what happens in the camera!
