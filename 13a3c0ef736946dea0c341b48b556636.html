The News

Federal Court says Apple's App Store can't be the only way to install apps on an iOS device

Australia's Federal Court handed down its judgement in the Epic vs Apple case and it's good news for Epic. For starters, they're going to bring back Fortnite to the App Store now that the judge said Apple's termination of Epic's developer account was bullshit and that Epic (and I assume anyone else?) is allowed to use alternative in-app payment systems. But much more useful for those of us who don't play Fortnite, the judgement says Apple has until March 1st to allow other app stores to operate on iOS devices and make it possible to install apps direct from the web. Apple said "we respectfully disagree with many findings in this case" and "will request a stay and appeal the outcome". I wonder how they'll punish Australians or try squeeze the government to try and stop it happening like they have elsewhere in the world?

Spotify cracking down on family plan sharing & Plex sacked entire player team

Heads up for anyone sharing a Spotify family account with people that are not actually in the same house/family - Spotify has begun making "members on its Family Plan to verify they lived with the plan manager by scanning a QR code. This code appears on the plan manager's phone, which the plan member then needs to scan with their own phone". You've got 7 days to scan the QR code and if you don't, "won't be able to join another Family or Duo plan for 12 months" and have to buy an individual plan. Spotify said this is not a "crackdown" but a "routine improvement". Over at Plex, they've sacked the entire Player team and a salty Plex employee (former? current? dunno) posted to let us know and recommended using Jellyfin or Infuse instead. That post was quickly deleted but a screenshot lives on.

Katherine Bennell-Pegg has a good chance of visiting the ISS if we can find the cash

Katherine Bennell-Pegg has a real shot to be Australia's first astronaut. There have been other Aussie born astronauts, but they've gone to space as US citizens as part of NASA and the yanks paid for it. With Katherine, the European Space Agency has offered Australia a seat for her in a 2028 mission to the International Space Station for around $100m, and Australia's government "has been engaging extensively with the private sector, philanthropists, and other levels of government to raise funding to support a space flight". Apparently it's very rare for the ESA to offer their ISS spots up to countries outside the ESA, so this is a pretty big opportunity for the Australian space industry, even if it is pretty expensive with little direct benefits. God speed to Katherine, I hope it happens!

Something I Saw On The Internet

Three cheers (and some money and respect) for open source volunteers like Ondřej Surý

Charlie Warzel interviewed Ondřej Surý, a Czech guy that is the volunteer responsible for packaging up PHP for various Linux distributions. Old mate is surprisingly calm about it. He said it's mostly automated and he's been doing it for 20 years so for him, it wasn't that big of a deal. The short interview is worth reading in full, but I liked this quote from Ondřej: "When I started doing open-source, it was fun. And it kind of still is. But the environment has shifted. Open-source sometimes feels like exploitation. What I would like to see is to find a new model where people working on open-source would be awarded for their work and the hours and hours they put into it from the people who most benefit from their work. I would like companies to realize that this is exploitation. Any exploitation is not sustainable. And it will end up badly if this continues, because the people doing the open-source will stop being motivated to actually do anything in the end".
