The News

29 US states sue Meta claiming it knew it was bad for kids & ignored effects

A huge US court case has kicked off today, with 29 states alleging that Meta "developed features to hook children, that it misled the public about the dangers and that it violated kids privacy". The court case is trying to find out if Meta "deceived the public", i.e: it knew Instagram and Facebook are addictive and harmful and lied about it. Meta, of course, denies everything, and even made a mini website explaining the "safeguards" it added to Instagram and Facebook and that actually, Meta's products are very useful for teenagers. If Meta is found guilty, it could be on the hook for US$200b in damages (almost its entire 2025 annual revenue) and be forced to make changes to the platform. Good luck to the attorney-generals, anything that kicks Meta in the balls is a Good Thing.

Google buys up bankrupt airline's data to train LLMs

US airline Spirit is bankrupt and is selling everything it can to repay debt. They really do mean everything, as Google paid US$10m for "100 million emails, 500 million Microsoft Teams chats, 7.2 billion records for competitors' flights, 7.5 billion passenger transaction records from 2008, and more than 175,000 employee records from 1986", along with "information on revenue, aircraft operations, employee productivity records, audits and fraud, marketing campaigns, human resources records, project management, and pricing curve data" - but not "personal data, including the 97.5 million passenger profiles that the airline kept or the 50.2 million customer records from the Free Spirit loyalty program". Google said said they plan to use this data for training LLMs. Oh the irony of training an LLM on a bankrupt company that was infamously loathed by its customers.

State sponsored LLM poisoning has begun

Responsible Statecraft (apparently it is a serious news outlet) is reporting on an Israel government funded think tank called the Hanover Institute that churns out articles with a pro-Israel slant. Nothing new there, sockpuppeting is an internet activity as old as the internet itself. What's new however is how it's making content designed to appeal to LLMs scouring the the web to feed its knowledge on certain topics. The company responsible for these articles says it does "AI Story Optimization" and crafts the reports, articles and other information for "how LLMs evaluate credibility". It all sounds very grim, particularly when attached to manufacturing consent for a genocide, but it's just a new form of SEO, because most people don't use search engines now, they go to an LLM chatbot instead.

Something I Saw On The Internet

If you need the absolute fastest LLM inference possible, check out the Cerebras CS-4

Looks like Nvidia or AMD aren't the only game in town if you want to offer LLM inference, with Cerebras announcing the CS-4, "a revolutionary rack-scale solution that delivers up to 30x faster inference compared to GPUs", with its marketing material claiming it can deliver 1,000 tokens per second with massive 10 trillion parameter models. It appears to achieve this by delivering a whopping 43.2PB/s of memory bandwidth thanks to the 44GB of SRAM (not DRAM) on its WSE-3 Turbo chip - that's way more bandwidth and capacity than Nvidia's equivalent inference accelerator. What's interesting to me is that the CS-4 isn't a graphics card souped up to do LLMs, but a dedicated system just for LLM inference. OpenAI is already using it to offer its flagship model, GPT-5.6 Sol, at up to 750 tokens per second for "select customers".
