The News

North Korea stole over US$6b of cryptocurrency from Binance

Binance - one of the world's largest cryptocurrency exchanges - got hacked late Friday arvo, with over US$6b in cryptocurrency (Ethereum mainly, but some Bitcoin and Solana too) stolen. The FBI confirmed it was North Korea, publishing a list of wallet addresses linked to the "TraderTraitor" hacking group. Binance is blaming "a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop, which allowed an attacker to gain access to a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions". Binance froze all withdrawals immediately and have just begun allowing withdrawals again "in phases" and are claiming they've "replenished" 90% of the stolen cryptocurrency, promising to "absorb the financial impact of these stolen funds, not our users" by using the company's money to make everyone whole. Congrats to the North Koreans on another successful heist.

I can hear the AI bubble making popping noises

Alphabet's latest financials didn't go down well with the market, dropping 13% and losing almost half a trillion dollars in market cap. Why? Investors aren't convinced the astronomical sums of money Alphabet is spending on AI is worth it. Ditto OpenAI, who passed the hat around for US$30b of additional funding recently according to the WSJ, but only secured US$11b. Blackstone is telling its investors that Anthropic will receive a "junk" credit rating, which means if Anthropic wants to borrow money (to buy more GPUs, build datacentres etc.) the interest rates on it will be astronomical. This then led to a wide range of companies in the PHLX Semiconductor Sector Index, like Intel, Arm, Qualcomm etc, suffering 10% or more drops in their market caps as their current bloated valuations are all tied to the future prospects of selling more stuff to AI hyperscalers.

France wants to tax businesses per gigabyte of internet bandwidth

The French have cooked up a law to tax internet bandwidth at 0.055 euro a gigabyte. Most of the info is in French or on English news websites that I generally don't trust so details are sketchy to me, but the plan is that ISPs will "measure the data flowing to their customers and note which company sent it" and report that to the tax authority, who will then bill the companies, capped at 15% of their French revenue or 2% of its global revenue. The party that managed to get it passed reckons it'll raise over 10 billion euro a year. VLC, a very good app made by a non-profit based in France, posted to X that it'll cost them about a million euro a year based on their current bandwidth. The law hasn't passed their upper house/Senate equivalent yet and the actual government seems opposed to it (I don't know shit about French politics), so it's unknown if the law will come into action, but still, it's wild it even got this far to be honest.

Something I Saw On The Internet

Kids can't read because social media fried their attention spans

Tyler Jagt wrote a thing about how their students suck at reading. Why do they think it's become a real struggle for young people at a university to read 20 pages of text? Smartphones and generative AI are numbing our brains. From personal experience, I totally agree. I used to like TikTok and Instagram, endlessly scrolling through videos, telling myself I am watching this stuff so I can be connected to what everyone else around me is thinking, feeling and doing, but then I tried to read a book for the first time in years and it was physically painful to concentrate on the pages. I kept reaching for my phone and realised this is fucked, so put the phone in a different room and forced myself to read. It took a few weeks of perseverance, but now I can read a book again.
