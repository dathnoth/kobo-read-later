The News

25x math prize winners sign open letter saying there's a "severe misalignment" between AI and mathematics

25 Fields Medallists have signed an open letter declaring that "the push by AI companies to solve mathematical problems as a benchmark is detrimental to the science of mathematics, and to the mathematical community". The letter is short and worth a read, but the key point they're trying to get across is that while AI is going around "solving" all these math problems, it robs us of the ability to "develop understanding and the ability to formulate new questions and ideas", shooting ourselves in the foot. It's math nerds doing it for a deeper existential, realising that the journey is just as, if not more important than the reward, versus the AI gronks that see a problem, point their agents at it and use their victory as marketing material for how awesome AI is, ignoring the collateral damage.

Leaked docs show govt floating two bad options regarding AI company access to artist work

Cam got his hands on a leaked document showing the government wants to test how much they can water down copyright laws for the creative industry before they starting whinging too loudly, that also lets AI companies scrape their work. The article has screenshots of the two options presented to rights-holder lobby groups (thank you Cam!) so I won't repeat them in detail here - but both options put the onus on artists to "digitally protect material online if they don't want it to be used in training without a licence". Smells to me like a shit sandwich for artists. Meanwhile, the Australian is reporting that if Australian copyright law is loosened, OpenAI is promising to do model training in Australia, but if the laws aren't tweaked in their favour, they won't build new datacentres here at all due to the mandate for them to use renewable energy.

US Air Force confirms it has a space weapon, but won't say what it is or how it works

So, uhhh, the US military confirmed on the record that is has space weapons. At a conference in Washington DC, Air Force Secretary Troy Meink said "the United States now has on-orbit space control weapons capable of defending the joint force against hostile adversary action". There's no further details about what those weapons are, how they work or even when they were deployed, but Meink elaborated that "the space environment is critical for military and economic security, and we need to make sure that the US can operate freely there". The article explains how Russia has some sort of anti-satellite weapon and the USA thinks China has "highly maneuverable satellites, demonstrating an advanced level of technological and operational acumen that, if not already deployed for such purposes, could enable a formidable on-orbit counterspace arsenal". Just another thing to pile on to the already large heap of existential dread.

Something I Saw On The Internet

BYD cuts Aussie prices on its pretty good cheap EVs, Atto 1 & Dolphin

BYD reduced the RRP of their two smallest EVs in Australia - the Atto 1 and Dolphin. You can now get the little Atto 1 for $19,990 drive away, making it not only the cheapest EV in Australia, but the cheapest new car regardless of fuel type. It's only got 220km of range so it won't suit everyone, but my first EV (which was almost 3x the cost back in 2018) had the same level of range and was still very useful! BYD also dropped the price to $28,888 on the slightly larger Dolphin, which has 340km of range - a very practical amount of range. My EX30 has been capped to 70% max battery capacity all year due to a battery recall, which works out to around 323km of range and it really hasn't been an issue at all for me living in a "regional" (50km from the Melbourne CBD) town.
