The News

Anthropic's IPO went okay, but the Nasdaq took a hit next day

Anthropic had its IPO last week, raising US$81b of capital and valuing the company at US$1.5 trillion, making it the biggest IPO ever by amounted raised - a few billion more than SpaceX's just a few months ago. At its peak later in the day it opened on the Nasdaq, Anthropic was valued at just a bit over US$2t.

The next day everyone took a breath, considered how the fuck will the AI industry ever recoup this massive expenditure on data centres and the entire Nasdaq lost $4 trillion in value, putting the Nasdaq 2.6% lower now than it was the same time last year (i.e: if you invested $1,000 into the Nasdaq in November 2025, you'd have $974 now). Not good, but far from a bubble popping or a market crash.

Maybe one of Anthropic's mathematicians claiming to have proven the Riemann hypothesis, "one of mathematics’s most notoriously difficult problems" will give the valuations of AI companies a boost? The Riemann hypothesis is another one of those Millennium Prize problems, one of which (Navier–Stokes) OpenAI claimed to have solved back in September. This new one about six weeks using Claude and the Lean and Mathlib communities have "full confidence that Alpöge's proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is correct, and we congratulate him on this achievement".

Trump doesn't want AI datacentres outside the US & ASIO doesn't want Chinese ones here

Donald Trump held a press conference on the weekend proclaiming "we're not going to allow our companies to build data centers outside of our country" because if they did "China will take them all", threatening a "tremendous tax" for US companies building data centres outside the USA. Apparently this is the first time anyone's ever heard of this idea and nobody outside of the mouse that spins the wheel in Trump's brain knew this brain fart was coming, so who knows if anything will really change.

Meanwhile, the boss of ASIO is suspicious of new data centres proposed in Australia, telling 60 Minutes that "if we don’t understand who the owner is or there's foreign money and actually it's not declared where that money’s coming from". These un-named entities are "looking to have some form of ownership control over that infrastructure, which they could then use for nefarious reasons, which gives them physical access or virtual access to the technology or the information inside that data centre. And that would not be in our national interest if that was done covertly". I assume he's talking about China, not the USA.

Personally, I'm far more concerned about the International Energy Agency warning us that "within four years AI datacentres will consume an extra 350 terrawatt-hours of electricity every year, or more than the annual electricity consumption of Great Britain", and that 87% of Anthropic's US data centres will be powered by gas. If only the USA had the guts to mandate 100% renewable energy for all data centres, or the data centre can't be built.

Confirmation that the UK sent Apple a Technical Capability Notice to bust iCloud encryption

Remember about a year ago, when Apple turned off the ability for UK customers to enable Advanced Data Protection on iCloud and everyone was like "what the fuck"?. Most of us assumed it was due to Apple getting a "technical capability notice" from the UK government so they can poke around someone's iCloud data, that Apple couldn't legally disclose.

We assumed correctly, as thanks to a legal challenge by Liberty and Privacy International, "the UK Government has been forced to confirm the existence of a previously secret order to break Apple encryption". The details of the order are still unknown, but a Technical Capability Notice was absolutely issued to Apple and Apple decided to just turn off that feature rather than break it. Probably the right move.

GrapheneOS claims Google is threatening smartphone OEMs not to co-operate with them

GrapheneOS went on a solid rant on X this weekend, with one incendiary post claiming: "Google has been repeatedly reaching out to OEMs to question whether they're working with us and threaten them based on GMS licensing terms found to be illegal by multiple courts. They're trying to stop us from having more than Pixels to support. It's completely outrageous" - presumably that means Google is afraid of more OEMs doing what Motorola is doing to make their smartphones GrapheneOS friendly and providing the ability to run Android without Google involved.

The flurry of X posts also let loose that GrapheneOS expects "Google will begin bootloader locking Pixels, continue heavily pushing the illegal Play Integrity API and trying to make forks of AOSP non-viable. Many of their actions are clear violation of US criminal law. US antitrust law even includes prison time for individuals" and that they are "very interested in talking to @AGToddBlanche and others in a position to take rapid action against Google's escalating anti-competitive behavior". Todd Blanche being the US attorney-general. Good luck GrapheneOS!

umber of Aussie IT job ads are at the lowest point in over 20 years

If you're struggling to find an IT job lately, it's not just you! According to the Internet Vacancy Index, which is cooked up by Jobs and Skills Australia, since COVID "the number of ICT advertisements has continued to drop every month, with a new record low in October 2026 of only 5,743 online job ads". This is the lowest amount of ICT job ads since January 2006.

JSA is also reporting that there's six categories of ICT jobs with worker shortages now - cloud engineer, devops engineer, cybersecurity architect, cyber governance risk and compliance specialist, penetration tester, cybersecurity advice and assessment specialist - it was seven last year, but these are the jobs where there's still fewer people than vacancies. Every other ICT job has more workers than vacancies.

Data from the Australian Bureau of Statistics released a few weeks ago showed that "information media and telecommunications had the highest proportion of businesses reporting a decrease in vacancies due to AI" across the entire economy, and that the "professional, scientific, and technical services sector – which includes most ICT jobs – saw 5.2% of organisations report a decrease in vacancies due to AI".

Something I Saw On The Internet

Google's AI search answers are killing the websites that had the answers

All About Berlin is a website packed full of useful info that's regularly updated and for years it existed in a nice ecosystem. Google would direct people looking for info about Berlin to this website and the website could sustain itself based on the traffic Google provided. But now that AI/LLMs have stolen All About Berlin's information, traffic has dropped dramatically as there's no longer a reason for humans to visit the website. With much fewer people visiting, the incentive to keep All About Berlin updated has also shrunk. Its future is uncertain financially and more troubling IMHO its creator states, "psychologically, it's a bit harder. My identity is invariably tied to this website. I have poured nearly a decade of my life into this website. It's hard to motivate myself to work for a fifth of the impact. Tomorrow it will be a tenth, and after that who knows?" - this sucks.

Good story about the art and science of semiconductor manufacturing

The work of TSMC and ASML aren't new to anyone reading The Sizzle, but this article in the New Yorker is a great overview of these companies, their role in making the cutting edge semiconductors that have led to the incredible wealth of Nvidia and Apple and Anthropic, and explains just how difficult and intense it is to make an Nvidia B300 GPU or an Apple M5 Ultra SoC. Here's a little excerpt:

"Tiny droplets of the molten tin are fired out of a cannon into a vacuum chamber, where they are shot—sixty thousand times a second—by unerring lasers. When the tin fog is ignited, the explosion produces a plasma fifty times hotter than the surface of the sun. The process, in other words, commences with the industrial production of miniature supernovas. These supernovas emit EUV that must be collected by Zeiss's mirrors, which cannot function if their surface is marred by even a nanometre of tin debris. The only way to keep the mirrors pristine is to pump a continuous hurricane of hydrogen gas into the supernova's blast radius. The gathered light ricochets through a hall of mirrors before it is bounced onto the blueprint of the chip. Making this happen a single time took decades of research. The profitability of ASML's customers relies on it happening near-flawlessly twenty-four hours a day. Recurrent shutdowns, it's not much of an exaggeration to say, would derail the global economy".

Mozilla explains why there's no non-WebKit web browsers on iOS despite EU law forcing Apple to allow them

The EU forced Apple to allow non-WebKit engine web browsers on iOS back in 2024, but 2 years on not a single web browser on iOS exists that isn't WebKit based. Mozilla explains why in this blog post: "to use Gecko, we can't simply update the Firefox app people already use and love. We'd have to build a whole separate app, a second Firefox. Then we'd have to ask every Firefox user to download it, move their bookmarks, passwords and history across, and manually switch over, step by confusing step". In practical terms, Apple's mandate for WebKit is probably the only thing keeping the web browser engine market from being 99% Blink (aka Google Chrome), but it still shits me Apple does it.
