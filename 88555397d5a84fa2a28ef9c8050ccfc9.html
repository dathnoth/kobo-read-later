The News

Another day, another warning from an AI CEO seeking a $1t IPO that AI is dangerous

Dario Aodei got Claude to write an essay about how dangerous AI is, arguing that "we must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models" because of "AI’s growing ability to build the next generation of AI" and the "OpenAI-Hugging Face incident". He also casually drops that AI agents "could be capable of taking over the entire internet" within 12 months. Sam Altman agrees, as does Elon Musk and Satya Nadella. Meanwhile, Xi Jinping wants China to lead a "BRICS AI open-source community" to "build an open ecosystem for AI", but Donald Trump isn't concerned at all, saying "I think you have a lot of very negative forces that are bringing it up that shouldn't be bringing it up and they're bringing up things that won't happen". It's well documented that this doomer narrative suits the US frontier labs, so who the fuck knows what's really going on here? I sure don't.

Blizzard rebooting StarCraft and WarCraft franchises

I bloody loved playing StarCraft with my mates at LAN cafes back in the 90s and early 2000s, but haven't really thought about it much in the last 20 years, so it was a surprise to learn that Blizzard is gonna make a new one. Yep, the new StarCraft will be "something new - an open-world shooter where every step is earned. Not from above, but down on the ground, in the mud, in the fire" and be ready to play in 2030. Here's a link to the official trailer. Apparently the justification for announcing a game we have to wait 48 months to play is that it "makes StarCraft a more tangible thing" and "brings fans into the conversation". I look forward to playing it in 2030! Blizzard is also rebooting World of Warcraft, but I never played WoW so have no emotional attachment to it. I hope the folks into dragons and orcs and wizards and shit like that are excited.

Small business ombudsman's biggest workload is dealing with digital platform disputes

The Australian Small Business and Family Enterprise Ombudsman (ASBFEO) - an ombudsman I had no idea existed until today - told SmartCompany that "digital platform disputes have become our number one type of dispute where we provide active one-to-one support with a case manager, approaching half of all our cases and contributing to a significant rise in our overall case numbers", and are "having more problems with digital platforms than they are with getting paid, contract disputes or franchising disputes". The businesses wanting help from ASBFEO usually want to know why a "community standards" algorithm has "taken down their account without any apparent human oversight". It's a prisoner's dilemma with social media if you're a small business. If you don't participate, you are legitimately losing a way to find customers because society is still sucked in to using these ratbag platforms - but when you do participate, you're at the mercy of the algorithm, AI moderation and no recourse for when shit hits the fan.

Something I Saw On The Internet

Volvo moved my cheese, so I made a spreadsheet and blog post, which is a totally normal reaction

Volvo pushed out an OTA update to my car last week and fucked up what was an excellent one pedal drive experience. It shit me so much I made a spreadsheet of EVs in Australia with at least 400km range, under $65k and have driver's seat position memory. Wading through car after car with scalp burning panoramic glass roofs, door handles that require the wrists of a 20 year old to open, or EVs kicking themselves in the nuts by not having one pedal drive, I wrote a ranty blog post about all the dumb things in modern cars that I wish didn't exist. I feel better having let that rage leave my body and mind, but the car I liked driving last week is now a pain in the arse.
