Everyone is stupid

I recently wanted to read a Stephen King book from start to finish to get an idea of how a prolific writer writes. I chose The Shining.

What follows will be spoilers for the book and movie, but let’s be honest: if you haven’t read or watched it by now you’re not going to. So keep reading. Probably this post will be more worthwhile than either anyway.

In the book Jack is a former alcoholic teacher/writer who was fired for losing his temper and beating a student. So he finds a job as caretaker of a hotel in remote Colorado which shuts down every season because the snow gets to be too high and the roads can’t stay open. He shuts in for the season with his hot- and intelligent-sounding wife Wendy, and his five-year-old son Danny, who has what is called a “shine” which is a sort of telekinetic/psychic voice that sees the past and the future-ish.

Long story short, the hotel turns out to be literally haunted and has a mind of its own, and it wants blood. It kills guests by getting into their minds and making them crazy. The last caretaker before Jack, Grady, killed his wife and two daughters then himself. Jack does pretty well fighting his inner demons, but the hotel overpowers him and convinces him to kill his wife and son.

The ending of the book is such that Jack gets pretty close, but ends up being blown to smithereens by the exploding central boiler, which Jack had forgotten to maintain. Wendy and her son Danny escape, and they live happily ever after.

Now, the book I’d give a 6.9/10 for plot, and an 8.5/10 for general readability and writing. Respect to Stephen King, he is a good writer (as if it were in question).

I was eager to now watch the movie to bring to life this fantasy world I had been submerged in for the last week. The movie is somewhat iconic, directed by Stanley Kubrick (2001: A Space Odyssey and Alien) and stars Jack Nicholson. It came out in the eighties, and I must say, visually, it is utterly beautiful. But the plot. What in the actual fuck was done to the plot?

It’s not the same story at all. The characters have the same name, sure, and they are stranded in the same hotel. But besides that, the book and movie share almost nothing in common.

And the movie is a complete, utter disaster.

Now, here is where I get to the point of this essay, which is that the masses are stupid. And more stupid than one can reasonably expect.

In the movie Jack really has no prior history. He is just a psychopath from day one. The hotel is not literally haunted whatsoever. It’s Jack who is haunted. His wife Wendy is a buffoon. And Danny seemingly too. The “shine” is some annoying raspy voice Danny makes. And for whatever reason Jack just wants to kill these two motherfuckers, seemingly because Wendy keeps bothering him when he wants to write (if that were reason enough my family would be dead a long time ago lmao).

Now Jack does have some sort of mental episode where he dances with the spirits of the past, but it is ambiguous. And when Jack asks these hotel spirits about Grady, the previous caretaker who killed himself and his family, the spirits are confused. They’re like, wtf you talking about chief? You’re the caretaker. You’ve always been the caretaker. Jack is confused about this but has business to conduct, so he leaves it at that.

That business is of course killing his family.

Long story short, he tries to, but ends up freezing to death after getting lost in the outdoor hedge maze trying to chase after his son.

And the closing scene is where I lost it. This is when I understood with a firm finality that even great Hollywood directors have no fucking idea what they’re doing and can sell literal shit to viewers and the viewers will give it a 9/10 on IMDB:

The camera locks in on a black and white photo hanging on the hotel wall. The photo seems to be from a prior era and shows a group of people posing for the photograph. As the camera pans in, there is a subject in the center, and you slowly see that it is none other than Jack. He really was always at the hotel. Which seems to imply that, what, the whole beginning of the movie where Jack interviews for the job in present time and moves into the hotel in present time was some kind of, I don’t know, psychotic episode??

The truth is, that ending was COMPLETELY MADE UP. It was bolted on to the end of the script to FEIGN PROFUNDITY. I imagine viewers who watched the movie having never read the book walked out of the cinema thinking, wow, what a MINDFUCK. How absolutely brilliant! What did Kubrick possibly mean by that, that bloody fucking genius!

I’ll tell you what he meant by that: ABSOLUTELY NOTHING. Kubrick reached into the spice cabinet, grabbed “profound obscure ending” and splattered it onto the end because WHY THE HELL NOT. Let the fucking viewers deal with it!

And the viewers launched onto their feet in applause.

Fucking stupid people.

Now, I’m no Stephen King apologist, as The Shining is the only book of his I’ve read. But I’d never seen so starkly, so plainly, how made-up everything is, and how with good enough packaging, turd can be sold as gold, and the masses will eat it up as brilliant. The movie has an 8.4 on IMDB! That’s really high!

I see this everywhere. There’s a new YouTuber who a couple of my viewers shared with me who makes AI-skepticism videos which seem to have titles suspiciously similar to mine, and which contains scripts which seem to be written by AI. I looked at the comments and I was surprised to see how quickly people latch onto new personalities. On the one hand this is a good phenomenon, because I was a new personality, and people’s propensity for the parasocial created that opportunity. But they were all giving the same positive praise they’ve given to me in the past—word for word! And I was like, wait a minute, you guys just say this to everyone!? Hahahaha. (I did literally laugh out loud.)

I think the masses are stupid. But let me be clear: it’s a bit strange. I have never met these stupid people myself. It’s not that a bunch of stupid people exist and congregate and their stupidity becomes some kind of murmuration. Oddly, each of these are individually probably not too stupid. It’s just that when they come together in mass, they are stupid.

I have probably been in some stupidity congregation myself without realizing. Have I not chosen to buy one brand of water over the other in the past?

There is more context to this than these two incidents. I’ve been reading many books and watching many movies to better understand storytelling. And I find that most stories are stupid. Even Harry Potter—now bear with me. Put down your pitchforks. It’s great fun. It really is. When I get tired of reading good books, I’ll pick up Harry Potter because it’s an incredibly entertaining read. But reading it as an adult, compared to reading it as a kid, I’m amazed at how simple it is. As a kid you remember some epic tale that is incredibly high resolution and complex. But reading it now, I’m like wait a minute, this is a damn kids book! It’s super shallow! The climax, the depth, the philosophy, the profundity, all come in at like five pages at the end where Harry has a conversation with Dumbledore. And the book ends.

Good stories are rare. The authors and books I have revered all turn out to be doing a simple-thing-scaled-up; describing scenery to get to one very simple plot arc that is not wholly substantive.

True genius is exceedingly rare.

Most day-to-day commerce consists of stupid creators making stupid products for stupid customers.

And here I am on the sidelines, waiting for genius to strike. Waiting for an utterly brilliant idea.

When all I have to do is bottle water and slap a provocative label on it.
