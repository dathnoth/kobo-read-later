The News

NSW set to give cops powers to plug your device into a Cellebrite without a warrant

Sydney Criminal Lawyers has given the fine folk of New South Wales a heads up that cops might soon have expanded powers to "scan and download" the contents of a smartphone during "routine arrests, roadside stops and other lawful detentions". This isn't just unlocking the phone and having a peek, it's plugging it in to a Cellebrite device and forensically dumping the entire contents of the device so it can be sifted through whenever they like, by presumably, someone who knows what they're looking for. According to the blog post, the cops could do this "during policing operations that were serious enough to require a warrant", but if this law is introduced, police can do it whenever they feel like, no warrant required. All the more reason to run GrapheneOS (or at the very least, the latest version of iOS) if you're at risk of random cops sticking their nose into your business. Thanks MMC for bringing it to my attention!

News Bargaining Incentive got a revamp, still no ETA on when it'll be legislated

The News Extortion Scheme Bargaining Incentive (NBI) got a few tweaks before legislation is passed some time this year. Social media or search companies that pull in more than $250m of revenue from Australia will be hit with a 2.5% levy (it was 2.25% on all AU revenue) on their Australian advertising revenue unless they enter into deals with media companies to "compensate" them for using their content (i.e: paying for the privilege of allowing links to news sites). They also changed some wording so Linkedin isn't classified as social media (it's a professional networking site now lol) and setup a grants program for media outlets with less than $150,000 in revenue. AdNews had a good interview with a smaller publisher about how the NBI impacts them and explains how the NBI is pretty much designed to benefit Nine, News Corp and Seven West Media. Of all the internet laws I've seen the Australian government run up the flag pole in 10+ years of writing this newsletter, the NBI is the sleaziest of them all.

EU has new AI transparency laws that I'm sure the usual suspects will ignore

As of August 2nd, businesses in the EU have some interesting obligations when deploying AI systems. From the EU's website: "Providers must design AI systems in a way that ensures individuals are explicitly informed whenever they interact with an AI system directly and add machine-readable marks to enable the detection of AI-generated or manipulated content. Deployers of AI systems must inform individuals when they are exposed to emotion recognition and biometric categorisation tools, deepfakes and text publications on matters of public interest without human review or editorial control". There's also a bunch of labels companies need to add to their AI systems so people can tell when AI has been used. Here's a press release from the European Commission with some more info. The premise of the laws are good, I like transparency around the use of AI, even if enforcing it seems impractical on all but the most egregious examples of AI abuse.

Something I Saw On The Internet

BMW showing ads for Spiderman movie in cars is supremely lame

I thought this was a joke when I first saw it on social media, but no, BMW really is showing ads on the iX3's infotainment system for Spider-Man: Brand New Day. Go look at the short video in the linked article's body. It's fucking ridiculous. BMW is a luxury car brand and they're showing ads for kids movies in the car??? This quote sums up how I feel about it: "Why is the ownership experience of a BMW the same as taking the cheapest tier of a streaming service, where you have to watch ads?". I don't know if they're doing this in Australia, but I bet they're gonna fucken try sooner or later, and it won't just be BMW - all the car companies already sell Australian vehicle telemetry to whoever wants it, displaying ads on that big colourful screen you can't avoid looking at is too big of a temptation to resist.
