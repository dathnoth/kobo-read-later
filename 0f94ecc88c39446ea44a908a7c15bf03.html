The News

Slow news day Apple dump

Very slow news day today, so here's a dump of Apple news that otherwise I wouldn't mention, but when lumped together is mildly interesting. The iMac and 14-inch MacBook Pro got bumped to have the M6 CPU. All the various Apple operating systems got updated - mostly bug and security fixes, but there sure is a lot of security fixes (263 CVEs in macOS!) so I wouldn't wait to install them. iOS 27.2 brings Apple Pencil support to the iPhone Duo, which is pretty cool, but only with one specific Apple Pencil version, the others won't work. Apple Creator Studio got some love, including MCP support in Keynote, Pages and Numbers so AI agents can do their thing and Keynote now has "support for Keynote Interchange Format (.keyx), an open JSON-based bundle file format designed to be readable and accessible to external tools, developer workflows, and AI agents" and according to John Gruber, a Python library to handle keyx files.

Victorian network of bushire detection cameras to expand

The Victorian state government will fund $3.8m over 3 years to expand a network of cameras out in bush that detect signs of fire and alert firefighters to investigate. The network of 7 cameras, which will be upgraded to 11 all up, "provide a 24-hour digital lookout, scanning the landscape for signs of smoke and fire and flagging potential fires for further checking". A total of 1.4 million hectares of land will be monitored by these cameras, which are installed in private plantations across the state. Here's a slideshow from 2024 by company that maintains tree plantations, with more details on the system. Apparently the cameras have 360 degree views out to 20km, with fires making up slightly over half the alerts they received during the trial period back then. I assume things have improved since then, with higher quality cameras and much higher quality detection algorithms.

Something I Saw On The Internet

Afolio is a Mac app for hardcore LEGO fans

Do you have so much LEGO in your possession that managing all those sets is a burden on your otherwise care-fee life? Afolio can help. It's a native macOS app (thank the Lord for more native apps) designed to "track your LEGO set and minifigure collection, browse the full catalog, monitor market prices, and manage inventories". It's free to start off with, then a once-off US$20 purchase once you get past 25 sets. Looks really well done IMHO and is made in Adelaide, which adds a little extra icing to this plastic brick-filled cake. I wish I had the problem this app solves, but I abstrain from LEGO, otherwise I'd go broke buying all the sets that interest me.
