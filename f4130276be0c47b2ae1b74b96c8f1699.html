The News

Google added AI image generation to Google Earth then took it away the next day

Google thought it would be fun and whimsical to allow anyone to type a prompt then get their dumb idea superimposed onto anywhere in Google Earth, presumably to share it on social media for shits and giggles. The day after "Nano Banana 2 in Google Earth" went live the blog post was sheepishly updated - "we've seen geospatial professionals using this feature for a range of useful purposes, however we've also seen people sharing screenshots of generated imagery that appear to violate our policies. So we're rolling back this feature in Google Earth while we work on implementing stronger guardrails". If you're unsure why this is a bad thing, this blog post explains why fake images of bombed hospitals, refugees crowding border areas, or airfields full of strategic bombers, might have negative ramifications in the social media environment of 2026.

eSafety reports that banning kids under 16 using social media made very little impact

Late Friday arvo, eSafety dropped a report on the efficacy of banning kids under 16 years old from social media. From the report: "our findings suggest that three months after the commencement of the age restrictions, reductions in children's use of age-restricted platforms and account ownership have been limited in scale". There has also been "a decline in parental awareness of children's social media use" and "an increase in use of certain age-restricted platforms as well as other social media platforms, and an increased frequency of use of online video games and, especially among girls, messaging platforms". Nice work everyone! A pissant assistant minister was sacrificed to respond, but only gave the usual "we don't get 100% compliance out of minimum drinking age laws, but it's still appropriate that we have that law on the books" excuse for the law's lack of ambition to actually do anything about the problem.

AppleCare One is now in Australia and might be a good deal

AppleCare One is now available in Australia. For $30/m you can have 3x devices (e.g: iPhone, iPad & Apple Watch) covered by the AppleCare extended warranty rather than having to buy them all individually and upfront. Slap on extra devices for $9/m. Paying $39/m to cover an iPhone 17 Pro Max, 16" MacBook Pro, iPad Pro and an Apple Watch Ultra for example, is way cheaper than getting it all individually. I don't really like AppleCare and have never purchased it with any of my Apple devices but a nice bonus of getting AppleCare One is that it includes loss and theft coverage and as Jonesy_sa on the OCAU forums points out - "you can add devices that are already up to 4 years old (or 1 year for AirPods/headphones), provided they pass Apple's diagnostic check and are signed into your Apple Account". No more having to buy it within the first 60 days or the opportunity to add AppleCare goes forever. Something for my fellow Apple Stockholm syndrome suffers to chew on.

Something I Saw On The Internet

Microsoft said they'll make Windows 11 be less crap on PCs with 8GB of RAM

Tucked in towards the end of a blog post from Microsoft about improvements they've made over the last 4 months to Windows 11 as part of their "commitment to Windows quality" (this should be the definition for "oxymoron" in the dictionary), is a little glimmer of hope - a mention that they're working on "memory optimization for 8GB and above. Reducing Windows memory footprint to deliver a fast and responsive Windows experience across the PCs customers use every day". It wouldn't surprise me if this is a direct response to a story in The Verge that tried to use Windows 11 on a Surface Laptop with 8GB of RAM and it sucked. As the forecast for RAM and SSD production looks incredibly constrained over the next 2-3 years, which means pricing will remain high, maybe it's time the industry takes a breath and does some optimisation instead of relying on Moore's Law to speed things up.
