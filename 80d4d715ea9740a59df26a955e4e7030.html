The News

Canva's CEO stares into the AI abyss and fires over 1,000 workers

Canva is firing 1271 of its employees, the bulk of them Australian. Co-founder and CEO Melanie Perkins published a blog post to explain that the mass layoffs are happening because "small changes won't be enough", that "we need to refound Canva" and that she is "deeply sorry". This is purely my personal speculation, but Canva is in a hard place right now. The basic design work they thrived on years ago is now the domain of Claude and ChatGPT. The margins on Canva are also getting squeezed by their heavy reliance on AI (provided by OpenAI) as that costs money every time someone clicks that "AI" button, unlike a traditional SaaS where the costs are pretty much fixed and you can scale up for marginal cost. Affinity, as nice as it is, they give away for free and is a loss leader for Canva subscriptions. They've wanted to IPO for ages but the conditions have never been right and it looks like they may have missed the boat.

High Court hears Reddit & Digital Freedom Project's case against under-16s social media ban

Reddit's legal case against the Australian government about the under-16s social media ban kicked off in the High Court yesterday. Reddit's beef is that they feel the law shouldn't apply to them as they don't meet the definition of a social media platform as outlined in the legislation and that the law "prevents teenagers from actively engaging in political communications through subreddits such as r/Australia". The Digital Freedom Project also sued the government about the same law, also arguing that banning kids under 16 from social media hurts their freedom of political communication, which contradicts the Australian constitution. Despite Reddit and DFP's cases are separate, the High Court is hearing them at the same time. The government's lawyers disagree and outlined the usual "harms" of social media (features like auto-playing video and non-stop scrolling that leads to addiction, which leads to lack of sleep, depression, etc.). The case is ongoing.

Another tranche of supermarket freezers hacked and huge quantities of food thrown in bin

More supermarket freezers across Europe got hacked and set to temps as high as 70-degrees Celsius, resulting in millions of dollars of food spoiled and disposed of. Supermarkets in Belgium, Sweden, Ireland, the Netherlands, Estonia and Finland were hit, pretty much the exact same way the ones in France were a few weeks ago. It's shameful that these supermarkets or the vendor that manages their refrigeration units, didn't do a mad rush to install VPNs on the remote management interface connections after seeing what happened in France. Even something as basic and cheap as Tailscale would solve this problem! Woolworths told the AFR their fridges and freezers aren't connected to the internet and Aldi said it has "measures in place". Coles refused to say what their setup is, so they're probably flapping about with their icy cold dicks in the wind, just waiting for anyone with an abliterated LLM to give them a nice warm squirrel grip.

Something I Saw On The Internet

We all hate "backseat software" yet it is everywhere

As professional or hobbyist computer touchers, I think we've all had a moment where we leaned back in our office chairs while seated at our little boxes of electrified sand and thought "what the fuck are we doing here? Why is this thing such a piece of shit?? It doesn't have to be this way!" whenever the computer does something bewildering to us. Mike Swanson noticed it too and wrote a poignant blog post about what they refer to as backseat software - "the slow shift from software as a tool you operate to software as a channel that operates on you. Once a product learns it can talk back, it's remarkably hard to keep it quiet".
