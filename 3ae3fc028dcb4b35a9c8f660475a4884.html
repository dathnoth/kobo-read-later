The News

All the state education ministers agree to a digital detox in schools

All the Australian state education ministers have agreed to a "digital detox" in state schools and the federal government will create an "Australian Schools Digital Detox Taskforce" to "provide advice on further steps to address excessive screen time and digital device use". The government reckons "evidence is showing us that the more time kids spend online the more likely it is that their results decline" and that "kids need to learn to think for themselves and there's some evidence that AI can undermine this by causing cognitive stunting". I don't hang around many kids, but if my strong and developed adult mind (some say it's the strongest and most developed mind in Australia) can succumb to the ills of cyberspace, the weak, undeveloped minds of children don't stand a chance. There's more background on why the government thinks this is a good idea in their Digital Detox paper.

Meta, TikTok & Microsoft seem to be ignoring Australia's News Bargaining Incentive

Haven't heard much about the News Bargaining Incentive (NBI) lately, but the AFR has an update on how negotiations between media companies and tech companies are going. Apparently Meta, TikTok and Microsoft haven't done anything and don't appear to be interested, so that means the Australian government will slurp up $550/m from those companies via a levy that they'll then re-distribute to local media companies. Also, the tech companies are shitty about this law and the local content quotas for streaming platforms (Netflix, Disney, Prime etc. have to make Aussie shows or they'll be fined) and have asked the US government again last week to "investigate possible breaches of the Australia-United States Free Trade Agreement". Will be interesting how much leverage US Big Tech can exert on its own government to influence our government, considering the big vibe shift in US politics.

Motorola's Signature 27 is the first phone to get official GrapheneOS support

Motorola's announced the first smartphone that'll be fully compatible with GrapheneOS - the Signature 27. The press release doesn't have a lot of information about the partnership, but it does say that it will support ARM Memory Tagging Extension, and a rep from GrapheneOS posted on Hacker News that Motorola is "helping us port GrapheneOS to their devices themselves and will be helping maintain it. They're also going to be providing us with the driver and firmware code we need and we'll have an opportunity to help harden it. It will be much better than Pixels when it comes to this. We won't have to do reverse engineering and won't be stuck with what's provided since we can get things improved or fixed". Excellent stuff from Motorola and I hope it filters down to cheaper phones (the signature 27 is a US$1600 flagship device) eventually. Will go on sale in the "coming months".

Something I Saw On The Internet

Kagi gives 50 free searches without an account so you can test the ad-free search engine

Kagi is my search engine of choice and whenever I mention it here (like I did yesterday) I get people telling me they'd love to try it out but don't want to make an account with Kagi just to give it a test run. Good news for those people, as "anyone, anywhere, can now visit kagi.com and search the web. No account required for the first 50 searches (100 more upon registration)". So if you've been wanting to give Kagi a try as Google continues to enshittify at a rapid rate, there's never been a better time! It's still the only search engine that's not funded by ads. Other alternatives to Google - like Ecosia and DuckDuckGo - are good, but still suck at Google or Microsoft's advertising teat. And yes, I know Kagi uses Yandex as one of their search result sources which isn't cool, but advertising shits me more than Kagi's minor use of Yandex.
