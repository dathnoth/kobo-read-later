The News

Anthropic is lobbying religions to consider that perhaps AI can suffer and has consciousness

The New York Times has a cringe inducing, but still fascinating, story about Anthropic's co-founder Christopher Olah trying to convince religions and wider society to believe that LLMs/AI might have consciousness and experience suffering. Yes, I laughed too, but the NYT talked to "20 religious and philosophical thinkers — Catholic, Jewish, Sikh, evangelical, Ubuntu and others — who have been involved in Anthropic's efforts". This really is a thing that's happening and I don't know if it's because Anthropic and the pro-AI mob genuinely believe there's a chance that their Nvidia GPUs have feelings, or they're deceitfully trying to manufacture consent for societal change that favours a business that'll make them rich. It could be both! Anyway, if you saw a headline in the news or social media about Anthropic lobbying the Pope, this article is where that anecdote comes from. Oh and Anthropic setup a lab recently to do biology research. Just wanted to throw that in here while I remember.

Data centre builder Firmus could be Australia's largest company after this week's IPO on ASX

Not a lot of news about this in Aussie tech media, but finance media is frothing over the upcoming IPO of Firmus on the ASX. If you haven't heard of Firmus, they build (or at least plan to build) datacentres around the world and if you haven't noticed, datacentres are very popular right now. They want to raise $7b at $11 a share this week, which would make Firmus worth $43.7b - putting it in the top 10 companies in the country, around the same as Telstra, Atlassian and Woodside. If it goes well for Firmus, there's every chance it could be Australia's biggest company by the end of the year. Their project in Tasmania, which will truck in water to keep the servers cool and will be the biggest power consumer in the state, has been in the ABC regularly, who have also reported on its co-founder Oliver Curtis who went to prison for insider trading. Nvidia is one of the major investors in Firmus, but the details of its investment I can't quite figure out and it smells like one of those funny money investments popular in AI infrastrcuture financing.

Apple to introduce further restrictions on macOS full disk access to handle reckless AI agents

Apple announced over the weekend that they're tightening controls for full disk access on macOS. It says "some developers are using Full Disk Access in ways that could put users at risk, exposing everything on their systems—including files, mail, messages, and even browsing history—without users’ full knowledge and understanding. For communication apps, this can also compromise the privacy of the people users are communicating with". They haven't said how they're going to limit full disk access, but do specifically mention that "as AI agents become increasingly capable and autonomous, the risks associated with this level of access will grow substantially" - so this is clearly aimed at Meta Muse, OpenAI Dots and the other agentic AI platforms. I get where Apple is coming from (news reports of Muse accessing iMessage without permission), but it sucks that even those of us that don't use AI agents suffer through more restrictions on our computers. I bet Apple's inevitable AI-agent won't have the same restrictions. Meanwhile, if you want to "turn off Apple Intelligence on macOS 27 and remove its downloaded models", this GitHub project can assist.

Something I Saw On The Internet

The idea of an ESP32-based watch interests me

I have no particular fondness for my Apple Watch, I simply wear it because it's the least annoying way to get a timepiece on my wrist that has the correct time on it with no effort (NTP sync via my phone) because Australia doesn't have a radio-based time sync service like North America, Europe, China and Japan. However, I am tempted by the LILYGO's T-Watch Ultra, a chunky ESP32 based smartwatch with GPS and LoRa radios built-in. It's very hackable due to the ESP32 platform, so I could make custom watch faces - something Apple doesn't think us plebs can be trusted with. Unfortunately it appears to be sold out. I hope it comes back soon so I can mess around with it.
