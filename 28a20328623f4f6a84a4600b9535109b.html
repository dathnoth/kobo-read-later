The News

Will eSafety do anything to stop the US govt execution live stream going viral on X?

The US government is going to live stream the execution of a mass shooter on December 3rd. That is something I didn't think I'd ever type, but here we are. Anyway, in Australia, we have the Online Safety Act, which would classify an execution as "refused classification", meaning the dissemination of it would be illegal. The eSafety Commissioner told the ABC "eSafety does not have powers to proactively prevent a live stream from occurring online", but does the ability to tell platforms to take the content down after the fact - but will it? Friend of The Sizzle Josh Taylor writes that it'll be a thorny political problem as no doubt X will keep it up and if eSafety tries to fight X (again) to take content down, Musk will whinge, Trump will hear it, and it'll further piss off the cosy with Trump US social media industry that's already upset about the upcoming Digital Duty of Care, which could lead to Trump punishing Australia some other way we might notice (PBS anyone?). Or we can just turn a blind eye to the violence like we always do.

The Australia Tax is back, Googlebooks are almost twice as expensive here vs the USA

Those new Googlebooks that run Android on "premium" laptops are starting to go on sale and Acer has priced theirs at $2,599 in Australia. Okay, whatever, who cares about an Acer laptop? Well in the USA it costs US$899, or $1,300 Australian dollars. Even if you add GST and a small percentage for currency fluctuations on top, Acer is still taking the absolute piss with an RRP of $2,599. When Information Age asked Acer what the fuck they're thinking, they were told "local market conditions and the positioning of the product within the Australian Googlebook category" is why we are getting slugged almost double. Acer isn't the only one, as Information Age points out that the other Googlebooks from ASUS, Dell, HP, and Lenovo also have huge markups here versus the USA. It's bad enough these devices are over priced due to RAM & SSD inflation, but to slap on a huge Australia Tax on top of that is even more stupid.

Hundreds of French supermarkets had their freezers & fridges hacked, loads of food destroyed

Mulitple supermarkets in France were hit with a cyberattack on their fridges and freezers, destroying a huge amount of food. Intermarche said that "around 150 of its stores were heated up to 50 C for six hours overnight", plus 80 of Carrefour's stores and 25 of Monoprix's had the same thing happen. That's a lot of ruined food! French cyber soldiers are investigating, but it's probably not a coincidence that in August a research team published details on vulnerabilities in the Danfoss AK-SM 800A refrigeration controller. Simultaneously, but unsure if related, a big Dutch supermarket chain has a "security issue" on its network that's closed all online orders - kinda the equivalent of Woolworths unable to do home deliveries because of a hack. Kinda scary to think that our food supply could be at risk due to the poor cyber security standards of supermarkets.

Something I Saw On The Internet

There's AI generated, open source clones of Microsoft Office and Adobe Creative Suite

Microsoft Office and Adobe Creative Suite are apps people love to hate and fair enough too. Once upon a time they were revolutionary, empowering even, but today they're bloated with features most of us don't use, keep draining money each month from our wallets and we are forced to use them against our will because everyone else uses them. It's no surprise then, that someone is trying to use AI to reverse engineer Office and Creative Suite and make it free and open source. Do they work? Yeah, kinda. Are they any good? Not really. Will it get better? Maybe. Should Microsoft and Adobe be worried? Perhaps. If I relied on Office or Creative Suite for my income should I bother with ArtCraft's knock-off versions? Nah.
