The News

Big telco industry changes on the table due to the 2025 Optus outage

The final report from a Senate inquiry about the big network bed shitting by Optus back in September 2025 was published yesterday and in it are so many major recommendations, that Mark Gregory's interpretation of it is "the best approach for the federal government may be to start all over again with a new telecommunications act that is better suited to the modern world". The report recommends Telstra stripped of its duty to run Triple Zero, creating a new statutory authority to run it instead, a "text-based service option for contacting Triple Zero in addition to voice calls", ACMA to create a "public register of compliant and tested mobile devices sold in Australia" and laws to mandate domestic mobile roaming for voice and SMS. All sound like good ideas to me. Lots for the government to chew on though, so don't expect anything before the next federal election.

Dell, Lenovo, HP etc announce Googlebooks pre-orders

They were announced in the middle of the year at Google I/O, but now Googlebooks are a thing you can pre-order and have in your hands early October. Googlebooks are laptops like Chromebooks, but instead of ChromeOS they run Googlebook OS - a flavour of Android that runs the massive collection of Android apps from Google's Play Store and is designed to "work seamlessly with Android phones". If you're cucked into Google's ecosystem the way some of us are into Apple's and you want Gemini jammed into every possible scenario Google's product managers could think of, then a Googlebook looks pretty damn cool. Dell, Lenovo, HP, Asus and Acer have hardware, with a mix of Intel x86 CPUs and Qualcommm ARM SoCs - Tom's Hardware has a good comparison of all the options.

Meta's Muse AI agent is banned by Amazon and the Mac version can be easily exploited

Meta launched Muse - a "personal AI agent" that "doesn't just answer questions, it actually does the work" and that Meta has built "from the ground up to be safe, secure, private, and widely available" - earlier this month and I didn't write about it because I wanted to pretend it doesn't exist, but today it's in the news again because Amazon blocked the AI agent from buying stuff. There's no specific reason given as to why, but presumably Amazon is scared its US$68b ad business (which is far more profitable than being a mere retailer) will be rendered useless if people just buy stuff using AI agents. Meanwhile, the Mac version of Muse is a security disaster, with a 0-day flaw and exploit made public that turns Muse into the "ultimate backdoor" and a very valuable target due to all the personal info and access to other accounts that users give Muse access to. Then again, if you actually installed an app from Meta on your computer, privacy and security probably aren't high on your list of priorities.

A game where you pretend to be a network engineer

There's a game on Steam where you "design and build network connectivity and services to clients in an ever-expanding randomly generated tower. Configure and maintain equipment to ensure you meet the requirements of tower dwellers. Manage your spending for expansion and daily operations". Does that sound like fun to you? Then you'd like Tower Networking Inc. It's in still in Early Access and costs $30. Reminds me a little of Factorio, a game I spent many hours playing and enjoyed but couldn't finish because I am too dumb.
