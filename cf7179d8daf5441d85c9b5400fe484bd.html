The News

Ukraine drone strikes destroy multiple Russian Yandex data centers

Ukrainian drones have blown up three out of five Yandex data centres in Russia over the last few days. Yandex being the equivalent of Google in Russia as western companies aren't allowed to operate (kinda like China), but also offering taxi services (aka Uber), on-demand food delivery (aka Doordash), dating apps (aka Tinder), LLMs/AI chat services (aka ChatGPT) and a plethora of other 3rd party services dangling off Yandex's cloud computing stuff. Yandex is an avid web crawler, their bots feeding Yandex search and Cloudflare noticed an almost 100% drop in activity from Yandex's network, with a small percentage appearing again in the last few hours. Yandex Cloud's status page is full of red. Imagine the carnage if a few drones flew into three of the 6 us-east-1 zones?

Bitwarden stops releasing client apps GPL, upsetting some customers

From an employee post on the Bitwarden Community Forums (my password manager of choice at the moment, self-hosted via Vaultwarden): "Starting in the next release, the Bitwarden apps published to the various stores will be the commercially licensed builds. No action is needed, and the apps will work exactly as they do today". They are still open source, but the client apps will not be GPL licenced. Deeper in the thread, same employee posts that "some future components will be published under the commercial license and will exist only in that build. Newly developed features will be evaluated on a case-by-case basis for which license applies to them" - which I interpret as the GPL version will have fewer features, which kinda sucks. Lots of people in the comments reckon this is the start of a long, slow "rug-pull" on the open-source aspect of Bitwarden. They're probably correct.

Microsoft dropping OneDrive storage limits included with 365 subscriptions

Also in tech companies winding back things we previously enjoyed, Microsoft 365 "Family, Premium, and Pro plans will move from an assigned 1TB of cloud storage for each user to a shared 2TB for all users" as of May 2027, resulting in "a subscription shared across six users will have its total cloud storage capacity slashed by 66.6%". The price is staying the same. I guess if some people are using fuck all and a single user is needs more than 1TB, this works out well, but if every family member was using a generous amount of OneDrive space, this change makes everyone worse off. Chalk it up to more collateral damage from AI, as not even Microsoft is immune to the growing cost of storage. If you haven't looked at HDD pricing lately, it's astronomical! I am not looking forward to the inevitable day a drive in my NAS shits itself.

Something I Saw On The Internet

The dolphin curve is the new duck curve

You've probably heard of the duck curve - "a graph showing the electricity demand remaining after subtracting electricity supplied by variable renewable energy sources (primarily solar) over the course of a day" - but have you heard of the dolphin curve? It's also a chart, but showing a hump when curtailment occurs, that is renewable energy going to "waste" because there's so much of it in the network, that the price goes negative s it's cheaper to stop producing energy than to keep paying to supply it. A strong example of the dolphin curve happened yesterday when the NEM hit a record of 17.4GW of rooftop solar output, which resulted in a total of 82.3 GWh of curtailment in the NEM over the course of the day - the bulk of it in VIC and SA. To put that into perspective, 82.3GWh is enough electricity to power NextDC M1 for 214 days (M1 uses 0.16GWh according to NextDC).
