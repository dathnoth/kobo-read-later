The News

GitHub's recent spate of outages is due to crazy high demand, probably from AI coding agents

GitHub shit the bed for 8 hours on the 17th of August, the second major outage this month after GitHub Actions were down for a few hours on the 6th of August. GitHub took to their blog to apologise and explain, with the usual corpospeak that I won't repeat here, but this section was interesting to me: "neither outage was caused by a code or configuration change. Both incidents were capacity failures at their core. We failed to scale critical components before demand exceeded their capacity. Since April, monthly commits have grown from 1.4 billion to 2.9 billion". In just two months, an already very popular and relatively mature service has double in activity! GitHub doesn't detail what they think is causing such exponential demand for their service, but it's gotta be LLM coding, right? Agents just hammering the git repos like no tomorrow.

Meta & Google must give cash to Aussie media companies again or govt will hit them with a 2.5% levy

The News Bargaining Incentive is now law, having passed both Houses of Parliament. It requires "large, digital platforms operating significant social media or search services, irrespective of whether they carry news content" give a certain amount of money to Australian news outlets, or be slugged a 2.5% levy. This was specifically done so that companies like Meta can't do what they did in Canada and just block news entirely and escape paying the media outlets. If they do that in Australia, the government will take 2.5% of their Australian advertising revenue and distribute it themselves to the media companies. Will be interesting to see what deals are done and with who. I've said it before, but it's tough to make me feel sympathy for Meta and Google, but the News Bargaining Incentive made it happen.

Stripe tells investors that the singularity started on Jan 1st 2026

Stripe - the company that takes a $2,698/yr cut of the $80,388/yr you all pay me to read this newsletter - spent US$7b to acquire OpenRouter, the company I pay when I debase myself and use an LLM. I wasn't going to bother mentioning this acquisition, but I saw Stripe's investor letter explaining why they decided to buy OpenRouter and had to say something because Stripe reckons "that January 1st marked thebeginning of the singularity, and we have since been operating on that basis". Uhh, okay? The singularity, if you don't know, "is a hypothetical event in which technological growth accelerates beyond human control, producing unpredictable changes in human civilization". The singularity probably isn't the sole reason why Stripe purchased OpenRouter, but to say that they're framing all their business decisions through the lens of not only the singularity happening (weird, but okay), but having already happened (????), is wild. This is the kinda shit that goes though tech bro minds!

Please enjoy this 1985 video about CSIRONET, "an Australia-wide computing network offering its services to CSIRO, government departments, tertiary institutions and private companies". It's the internet before the internet! Glorious 80s computing - plotters, acoustic couplers and even what I think is a 128k or 512k Mac.
