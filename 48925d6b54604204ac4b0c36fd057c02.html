The News

OpenAI fronts Australian senate inquiry, Wikimedia finds its own rogue agents, OpenAI does more math stuff

OpenAI sent their chief strategy officer Jason Kwon to appear at the joint select committee on AI and repent for their sin "hacking" of Australian government websites. It was pretty uneventful, nothing interesting to report besides the usual politicians asking questions and a sheepish dude in a suit sticking to OpenAI's talking points. Wikimedia made public that OpenAI's agents abused its websites too, making "edits to our wikis, some unsuccessful attempts to exploit a public note-taking tool we host, and heavy traffic". Over in the mathematics department, OpenAI published a GitHub repo with "722 manuscripts organized into 372 families" containing solutions to loads of long standing math problems. Does solving these math problems lead to any practical, applied outcomes that benefit humanity? Otherwise, what was the point?

Windows 11 26H2 is a "predictable and low-disruption update" rolling out now

Windows 11 26H2 was released last week and is starting to roll out to more users. Here's a list of things Microsoft reckons "IT pros" should know about the new update. Not a lot of user facing changes (sorry for the link to X), just "a smarter file explorer", "better Windows Search", some accessibility stuff and more "ways to make Windows your own". Microsoft themselves are describing it a "predictable and low-disruption update" that "brings together" all the little updates 25H2 got over the last 12 months into one big update. Probably the best thing in 26H2 is the lower RAM consumption, with WindowsLatest saying that "on my three-year-old 16GB RAM laptop, and right after a restart, Task Manager showed 7GB in use out of 15.7GB. Earlier, it would cross 10GB before I even opened an app" and the new Low Latency Profile manager helps speed up app launching.

Someone found a way to escape a KVM VM, Vercel paid out a bug bounty for it but we have no details about the 0day

Security researcher Paulos Yibelo posted on X earlier in the week that Vercel paid out a bug bounty to him for a "full VM escape zeroday (guest>host root in industry standard hypervisors)!", with Vercel responding to the post on X with "we've confirmed a KVM 0day through our Vercel Sandbox bounty program. Affecting the industry's gold standard solution for Linux virtualization". Yes it's as bad as it sounds - a way for someone with access to a virtual machine to get access to other VMs on the same host. KVM is used in so many places too. All the VPSes I run use KVM. My Proxmox homelab server uses KVM. Even Google and Amazon use KVM for their cloud services. We have no details on what the exploit looks like or even if KVM is aware of it and if a fix is coming.

Something I Saw On The Internet

Why not place a mini AI datacentre near EV chargers?

An EV charging network in the USA called Xeal is planning to install boxes at charging sites packed with 48x Nvidia GPUs. These "Latient Pods" leverage either the spare existing capacity at their sites or allow for upgrades where they already have permission. They've got 1,600 locations in mind and up to 200MW of electricity to play with. Xeal can then lease the compute to whoever wants it. The first Latient Pod will go online later this year. Honestly, it's a pretty smart idea considering the biggest problem for AI inference is electricity and they have loads of spare electricity. 48x Nvidia GPUs worth almost US$2m out in the open is asking for trouble though. We've seen what junkies do to EV charging sites for a few bucks of scrap copper, imagine what they'll do for a few dozen Nvidia GPUs!

Lunar Orbiter was essentially a flying camera.
