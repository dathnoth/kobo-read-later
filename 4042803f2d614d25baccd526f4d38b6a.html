The News

OpenAI to add text watermarks & Sam Altman says we need to "accept some bad things"

OpenAI is going to add "an invisible watermark to eligible ChatGPT and Codex text output in the European Union", in order to adhere with the EU's laws on being able to identify AI-generated content. Unlike Anthropic, they're only doing it in the EU, not worldwide. A "text watermark detector" will come later. Meanwhile, the boss went on a podcast and uttered the phrase, "one of the differences between us and some of the stricter AI safety people is that we believe that the world should accept some bad things happening for the benefits of this technology and people having the agency [to use AI widely]". Cool, can't wait.

Last weekend's F1 GP saw every car on the track slowed by a computer bug

During last weekend's Bahrain GP (which took place in Malaysia because of the US-Iran war) there was a software glitch that impacted all the cars on the track - "driving slowly behind the safety car with reduced hybrid power in some parts of the track due to the extremely wet conditions convinced several of the cars that they were being tricked into breaking a hard-coded rule meant to prevent cheating, triggering an automatic 60-second shutdown of their electric motors that caused chaos as car after car slowed for the one in front, then took varying lengths of time to reboot their systems". All I have say to on this topic is, lol.

Qualcomm now pays Huawei for patents as part of multi-year patent licencing deal

A new marker of how Chinese tech is growing in dominance has dropped, with Qualcomm and Huawei doing a deal to cross-licence patents. Huawei has paid Qualcomm a lot of money over the years for access to patents, mostly around mobile phones and wireless tech, but now Qualcomm will be paying Huawei more than what Huawei pays Qualcomm for the first time in 25 years. Interesting that Qualcomm's press release highlights Huawei's contributions to 5G tech, an area where Qualcomm was historically the big boss. They don't say that the specific patents are, but a Bloomberg article says it includes also Huawei's "novel LogicFolding chipmaking technique". Xi Jinping's Made in China 2025 plan is paying off nicely.

Something I Saw On The Internet

VidSrc is an impressive implementation of movie and TV show piracy

Sometimes I think internet piracy is a dying art. The kids don't know how to BitTorrent and they certainly aren't aware of the luxury automated piracy machine that is Usenet and an *arr setup. But then I saw something called VidSrc that takes watching your favourite movies and TV shows without paying a cent to a whole new level of ease. It's literally a piracy API you can embed into any website and it works fucking perfectly with all my favourite shows and movies. You just enter an IMDB or TMBD ID and it plays. I assume the target market for this API is to pop the code in a HTML page and monetise it with malware or residential proxies. If the media companies were smart, they'd do this themselves instead of letting the pirates have all the fun!
