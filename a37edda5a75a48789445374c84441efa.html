The News

An Australian AI Standards legislation and AI regulator is coming soon

Australia's getting "world leading" AI regulation with the planned introduction of the Australian AI Standards legislation. There will also be a new regulator specifically for AI safety that will "apply to all frontier AI companies that wish to operate in Australia" and will have the power to "enforce significant penalties for AI companies that fail to comply with mandatory obligations". Those obligations are:

  • Maintain security controls across all stages of development and deployment
  • Identify and manage serious risks associated with their models
  • Allow independent evaluation of their models
  • Provide mandatory notification of incidents to the Australian Government rapidly
  • Maintain and publish safety and security frameworks to ensure good governance, risk management and transparency
  • Respond to updated standards and rules as technologies change

Sounds good to me, as long as the regulator has a spine, unlike the eSafety Commissioner, who is all bark and no bite. Look at all the powers we gave it, yet nothing on social media or the internet improved and we'd be in the same position if it didn't exist. Henry Fraser, a law lecturer at QUT, thinks the Australian AI Standard's scope is too narrow and by focusing on frontier models, won't regulate other "algorithmic systems" integrated into many aspects of our lives.

House House admits to using a tiny bit of AI to fix a big in Big Walk

House House, the developers of Big Walk, disclosed to the Independent Games Festival awards (and posted to its website) that: "In the final stretch of Big Walk's development, LLMs were used sparingly by House House to help work through difficult technical challenges and bugs. In some cases, snippets from LLM responses were copied as a starting point to be modified into game code—we’d estimate that well under 0.1% of Big Walk's code had anything to do with this usage, and generative AI was not used in any other context when making the game". They do a good job IMHO of justifying the use, despite the negative externalities of AI.

This led to a statement signed by all the jurors of the awards, denouncing AI, declaring its use in any aspect verboten and refusing to vote for any games that use AI in any capacity. It also kicked off another round of debate about the use of AI in video games. The gaming scene is wayyyy more anti-AI than the broader IT/tech community it seems and has a lot more class solidarity than the little Ayn Rands in programming.

Google & eSafety Commissioner have opinions on the Digital Duty of Care

There's an inquiry into the Digital Duty of Care law taking place and Google told it that as the draft stands, Google could be required to check age of all Australian users and block anyone under 18 from "everyday communication services like messaging, email, and private file storage" to prevent kids from looking at porn. They want clarification that "when it comes to private communications and private file storage, there is not an expectation that we will be age assuring those users and blocking access to under-18s".

The eSafety Commissioner also fronted the same inquiry and when asked if the Digital Duty of Care will impact free speech, said "I haven't seen any indication, particularly over the past two years, that companies are over-censoring". The Commissioner also said that she wants "a private right of action and legislation tailored to increasingly violent and threatening online hate" added to the Digital Duty of Care. The ARC Centre of Excellence for Automated Decision-Making and Society agrees. I'm surprised we can't do that now, so it's wild to me the government seems reluctant to allow individuals to sue for harm, which is something we can do if most other companies harm us.

AI data centres are burning shitloads of gas, undoing decades of CO2 reduction progress

Today I saw in my RSS feeds a concerning concentration of news about the fossil fuel use of AI data centres: Anthropic's purchased US$3.4b worth of gas turbines that'll generate 1.7GW of electricity from Caterpillar. SpaceX wants to bulid a gas pipeline and gas power plant for a massive proposed data centre in Starbase, Texas. At COP31, BloombegNEF published a report explaining how there's "181GW of announced gas-fired generation proposed alongside data centers", 90% of them in the USA. Research published in Nature Climate Change claims that "coal power plants exposed to data centres built after 2020 exhibit significantly lower retirement risk" - i.e: data centres are keeping coal power plants running much longer than if the AI boom wasn't happening.

This is bad, very bad. Burning gas and coal for electricity is literally poisoning our air, during a climate emergency, for something relatively useless to our existence. In Australia at least, our governments are planning to mandate renewable energy for new data centres. That is not the case in the USA and nothing appears to be happening to stop it.

Something I Saw On The Internet

There's an official AWS emulator, totally made with AI and no human review

Amazon Web Services is a huge beast. There's dozens of services you can rent off them, each with their own idiosyncrasies, so an emulator or test area makes a lot of sense so people can muck around with it before spending real money or placing real data into AWS. Services like that exist (Floci, MiniStack, Moto & probably others) but now Amazon's gone and made its own official one - AWS Emulator. According to the press release it "was written entirely by autonomous agents" and "our team hasn't written or reviewed any of the code". They do explain how their team of agents is kept in line and checks against a whole bunch of stuff, and it is orchestrated by people I assume are very good at programming, so I wouldn't say it's "vibe coded", but yeah, welcome to the future of software engineering.

Logitech is giving all its staff a dedicated day off work to play video games

Logitech put out a press release to announce "Logi Game Day, a dedicated day off for employees to play games while formally recognizing the cognitive and professional benefits of gaming" - which happens to neatly coincide with the release of GTA6 on November 19th. They reckon that "we see firsthand how Logitech employees who sharpen their skills in-game translate directly back to work & problem solving" and "when people have dedicated time to log off, decompress and immerse themselves in play, they return with clearer focus, renewed energy and fresh approaches to complex challenges". It looks like the folks at Logitech can nominate the day they want to take off, it's not like the entire company gets to go home at once and play GTA6 for a day, but more like what many employers do with mental health days. Maybe send this press release to your boss as a little hint???
