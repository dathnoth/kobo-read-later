The News

Leaks show Proton CEO's "full AI psychosis" and "petulant" behaviour

Someone working at Proton - the Swiss company selling private and secure alternatives to "big tech" online services - got pissed off with their CEO's "full AI psychosis" that they dumped a load of internal chats and emails on 404 Media's doorstep. When people said that Proton should take a few days to pentest some new code, the CEO said "That's bullshit and loser thinking. Of course it's possible to be fast and secure", to "stop thinking always of how to be slower, and focus more on how to be faster", and to "stop lovingly handcrafting all our code and use AI more". That's just the tip of the iceberg and the articles show the CEO acting multiple times as a "petulant child" on different issues. Oh, and Proton is re-interviewing all staff to get rid of people that can't "translate AI's capabilities into better products, stronger execution, and new ways of working". Even the Hacker News crowd is pissed off and they pretty much love AI.

Reddit to block access to site unless you're logged in

Reddit's CEO made a post explaining why they're going to block people who aren't logged in from viewing Reddit even though "as someone whose life has been defined by the open internet, it's a big change for me". It boils down to two things. First, they want to push algorithmic feeds on Reddit, which means they need more people logged in and engaging to send signals to the algorithm, which they justify as "for users to get the best version of Reddit, it requires personalization", but it also happens to be the best thing for ad targeting (i.e: Meta's entire business). Second, they make a lot of money selling "human" interaction activity to train LLMs, so want to block scrapers, as well as maybe juice the amount of humans involved to keep bots out of discussions by requiring an account. No dates set for the change, but it's gonna happen eventually according to the CEO's post.

Fujifilm invests big in upgrading LTO tape factory due to fivefold demand

Fujifilm will invest US$63m to expand its Japanese tape factory to build more LTO-10 tapes that can hold up to 40TB of uncompressed data. Apparently demand has increased fivefold due to "artificial-intelligence-driven demand" and is the biggest investment Fujifilm has made in LTO tape manufacturing in 20 years. I'm not sure if it's AI companies wanting the tapes for their own purposes (are they actually backing up all the chat conversations/context???), but because the cost of HDDs and SSDs the AI companies do actually need have gone up in price so much, LTO tapes, which were kinda expensive as a holistic solution just a few years ago, are now suddenly much more financially attractive for other companies that really need backups, despite LTO tape being a bit of a pain in the arse to work with (ask me how!). This has also revitalised LTO tape development, with the main LTO players (IBM, HPE, Quantum) bullish on the future of the tech.

Something I Saw On The Internet

Federal government signs on to Tech Reuse for Good Charter

The federal government has signed the Tech Reuse for Good Charter. It is a "voluntary, principles-based initiative that encourages organisations to reduce their electronic waste by donating surplus digital devices well before the end of their practical life". Those donated devices will "help improve access to education, employment, healthcare and essential services for Australians experiencing digital exclusion, particularly families on low incomes, students, First Nations Australians and survivors of domestic violence". Many other large businesses have also signed the charter, like Aussie Broadband, Optus, Telstra and a couple of banks. You can find out more at the Tech Reuse for Good Charter website. If you've got any clout with a decent sized business, maybe agitate to get them onboard with this initiative?

Image Of The Day

After finishing my first Photon 2 lander, I couldn’t resist building a second one—this time with more personality and functionality. The highlight of this version is a crisp, full-color 1.9″ 170×320 TFT display from Adafruit, which makes the lander feel like a tiny retro mission console. Powered by the Particle Photon 2, this sculpture fetches local and global weather data, sunrise/sunset times, moon phases, humidity, and wind conditions, all in real time using Particle Webhooks and a handful of APIs. To add some flair, I included a buzzer that plays polyphonic tunes and a PDM microphone for potential voice commands. While the frame design remains faithful to my previous landers, the interface has been completely reimagined—now with smooth animations, retro-inspired gauges, and a polished GUI. (Mohit Bhoite)
