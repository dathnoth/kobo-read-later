The News

TIO wants to see stronger laws so telcos have to provide compensation for outages

The Telecommunications Industry Ombudsman (TIO) published a report this week about the "experiences of telco consumers living in regional, rural and remote Australia". There was nothing in it that most of us didn't already know - people outside the big cities have fewer options for connectivity and resolving faults take longer. What interested me however, was some of the TIO's recommendations, namely "Introduce individual remedies for significant delays in accessing mobile services" and "Establish SIP service standards, with remedies available to consumers when standards are not met". Basically, if there's an outage, give the impacted people compensation and/or don't bill them while they can't use the damn service. This article in the Conversation goes into a bit more detail on the TIO's report.

Apple and Google battling with court orders to open up their app stores

Last year Apple was told it can't have any fees at all for non-App Store transactions (in the USA at least). Apple appealed that, the court agreed there should be a fee for Apple to recoup "reasonable costs", and those costs are now up for debate. Apple reckons 5% to 15% (depending on the type of app) is what they need to "recover at least some compensation for the value that its IP-protected tools, technologies, and services provide to developers" - but hang on, isn't that what the US$99 annual developer fee is for?! Greedy fuckers. Meanwhile, Epic's lawyers showed in court how shit the process is to install a third party app store on Android, and the judge agreed, forcing Google there and then to make the changes within a week!

Honor's got a new phone that packs a flip out gimbal camera

Honor, a sub-brand of Huawei, is now selling a smartphone with a flip-out camera containing a full three-axis gimbal. When I saw the CGI renders last year I thought it was bullshit, but they're selling it now and there's YouTubers who have hands-on reviews. The phone is a little bulkier than an iPhone Pro, but not by much (9.6mm vs 8.75mm)! The camera in the gimbal isn't too shabby 4K/120 video out of a 200MP, 1/1.28" sensor that's optically stabilised and has with "Arri Image Science" support (10-bit 4:2:2 video using the Arri LogC3 curve) and integrates with Arri's other cinema camera workflows. They essentially took those DJI or Insta360 gimbal cameras and made it pop out of a smartphone on demand. Sells for around A$2100-A$2700 in China. What a time to be alive.

Something I Saw On The Internet

Please enjoy some 90s Australian CD-ROM art

Revisiting Australian CD-ROM Art of the 1990s is an online exhibition that, as the title explains, "presents ten CD-ROM artworks from the 1990s" using discs from various libraries and art galleries, on top of AusEaaSI (Australia's Emulation-As-A-Service Infrastructure). It's very similar to the Internet Archive's ability to play games or load software within the browser. The curatorial essay is worth a read, explaining the background of these CD-ROMs and why it's important to preserve this little slice of Australia's art history. Who knew Australia has a nationally funded emulation infrastructure??? (Thanks Rohan Pearce for sharing it on Bluesky!)
