The News

Apple leaks a bunch of new products, including AirPods with cameras

Apple leaked a heap of upcoming products in the latest beta of macOS Tahoe 26.7. Some are obvious, like the iPhone 18, new iMacs, iPads, etc, but there's a heap of "home accessories" like a Home Hub, Pebble, and a potential new HomePod mini. That's cool, but what really would have Steve Jobs spinning in his grave is a demo video of new AirPods with built-in cameras. Fucked if I know how they put cameras into earbuds, but Apple made a 13 second demo video and put it into a beta version of macOS. The "camera on the AirPods will feed information to Visual Intelligence, and Siri will be able to answer questions about the wearer's surroundings and log information. There is a direct reference to setting up Visual Intelligence on the AirPods". So Meta's pervert glasses, but even harder to discern if someone's wearing them?

NSW govt outlines data centre requirements to get fast-tracked permits

The NSW government appears to be first jurisdiction in the country with a holistic framework for data centre planning approvals, based on the Prime Minister's "AI in Australia's interests" speech. You can read the 23 page PDF here. It goes into detail about the water, power and noise requirements - like renewable energy PPAs for average electricity demand, "rainfall-independent water"water, testing of air pollutants from back-up generators etc - along with the ability to reduce electricity use by 25% for a 2hr period and much more. The deal is that if you're building a data centre and your plans meet all the requirements laid out in this document, you get fast-tracked planning approval within 75 days. It all sounds like common sense stuff to me and I hope, calms down the noisy group of Facebook users that freak out when a data centre is proposed near them, who often get scared by the reckless shit they see in USA data centres, like dozens of huge stinky gas turbines and damaging town water supplies.

More tales of personal computing becoming unaffordable

Here's a mixed bag of news relating to the downfall of personal computing. JB Hi-Fi's share price took a shit yesterday, recording its biggest single day drop in value ever. Aussies aren't buying as many gadgets as expected because the gadgets are full of RAM and SSDs and they cost too much! We're holding onto computers longer, which means less money for JB and The Good Guys. Meanwhile, gaming monitor manufacturers KTC and AOC are giving customers a heads up that prices are going up due to increasing production costs. KTC hasn't said what those costs are exactly, but AOC said it's due to higher costs for "panels, components, power-related hardware, and manufacturing". Tom's Hardware regularly tracks RAM pricing and their latest analysis shows that you could buy 128GB of DDR5-6400 for US$329 a year ago. Today it is US$3,400. 32GB and 64GB DDR5 kits of all speeds are up between 350% to 485% compared to last year. Cool.

Something I Saw On The Internet

AI companies are buying used books and destructively scanning them

There's been a couple of stories lately about second hand booksellers noticing a big increase in demand for their product, like these shops in the UK and Ireland. The hypothesis is that they're being purchased in bulk to scan and train LLMs on - a thing Anthropic has admitted to doing. Unfortunately, they are destroying the books in the process, but cutting off the spine and feeding the pages into heavy duty scanners, rather than using expensive, slow and convoluted machines to take photos of each page. 404 Media tracked a shipment of used books to an Amazon scanning facility where they do the same as Anthropic. I'd be a hypocrite if I said this is bad, but like, it sure would be nice if we could at least see the output of their scanning. Donate it all to the Internet Archive at least so they can have a physical copy and a digital copy to lend out? Or be cool and leave the files "accidentally" unprotected in an S3 bucket so Anna's Archive can find them??
