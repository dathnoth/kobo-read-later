The News

Coles decides not to renew contract with Palantir

Coles and Palantir have broken up. Their 3 year deal announced in 2024 comes to an end soon and it won't be renewed. No reasons given from either Coles or Palantir as to why. Maybe it was GetUp's campaign that used 700 digital billboards outside Coles stores to let people know about Palantir? Maybe it was ethical share-trading platform SIX launching a shareholder resolution "asking legitimate questions about data governance and human rights due diligence" that Coles didn't like the answers to? I'd love to see a post-mortem on what value Coles got out of Palantir. Was there any extra financial benefit and if so, was it worth the reputation hit?

Google "very pleased" judge won't make it divest ad-exchange platform

After Google lost its case against the US government for "monopolizing open-web digital advertising markets" in 2025, the Department of Justice wanted to Google to "divest its ad exchange and open-source some auction logic in its ad server". The judge in the case now thinks that's going too far, so instead "Google will be forced to adopt some behavioral changes", like "limits on self-preferencing tactics, data sharing with publishers, and nondiscriminatory treatment of third-party ad exchanges and ad servers". Google said they are "very pleased" with the decision. I don't know shit from fuck about the advertsing industry and Google's practical role in it, but it's never a good sign when a criminal found guilty by a jury (which is what happened to Google here) is "very pleased" with their sentence.

OpenAI's new GPT-6 Astra model is as intelligent as a human, apparently

OpenAI's new GPT-6 Astra model scored 99.9% on the ARC-AGI-3 benchmark, which attempts to measure the "residual gap" between current AI and AGI (artificial general intelligence) which the benchmark defines as "a system's ability to acquire any skill a human can, as efficiently as a human can". It's debatebale if this benchmark is even remotely analogous to AGI (looks to me pretty far off what a human can do, but what do I know?), but for US$19,000 of tokens and using custom harness, Astra managed to complete 99.9% of this AGI benchmark. A human did the same tasks for about US$13 and if you "only the brain's energy, and price it as electricity, the estimate drops to about 0.6 cents per session, or 0.067 cents per game attempted".

Something I Saw On The Internet

TCL's new P80 Ultra NXTPAPER smartphone has a nice display

Lots of gadgets coming out of IFA in Berlin right now, but the one that caught my eye the most is the TCL P80 Ultra NXTPAPER smartphone. It's a pretty ordinary smartphone (MediaTek Dimensity 7400 SoC, 50MP camera, 8GB RAM, Android whatever) but it has a really cool display. By default it's a 120Hz OLED but press a button and it goes into "Max Ink Mode", taking advantage of TCL's Nxtpaper tech to change to a "monochromatic, paperlike display; a custom Android UI with fewer apps and notifications; and dedicated software to use it as an e-reader" that enables "a full seven days of active use, or up to 26 days of standby time". I look forward to seeing one on display at JB Hi-Fi later this year, going "ooh that's nice" and keep using my iPhone because I am a prisoner to Cupertino.
