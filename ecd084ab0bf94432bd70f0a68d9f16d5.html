The News

Prime Minister Albanese visits Tim Cook at Apple HQ

Our Prime Minister met with Apple's executive chairman Tim Cook on the weekend in San Francisco for a photo opportunity and spent an hour "behind closed doors" discussing "strong and intuitive controls to help keep kids safe online" (Cook's X account) and "what we're doing to protect children from online harm and the work ahead" (Albanese's X account). Maybe a good first step to protect kids online is to lead by example and don't use a platform that does nothing to stop the spread of CSAM? Anyway, that was just a stop-over on the way to the upcoming United Nations General Assembly in New York, where the PM wants the USA and China to "cooperate" on AI because we need "to make sure that humans remain in control". Good luck with that mate.

ABC's fooling around with a BYD Shark highlights weak car cybersecurity standards

The ABC gave a BYD Shark plug-in hybrid ute to Fortify Labs in Canberra who mucked around with it for two weeks and were able to get remote access to the car. The article is very light on details, but the car "didn't even have a password" and they were able to turn off the lights, control the wipers, lock the doors, "blast music over the speakers", "play images on the BYD's giant infotainment screen" and worryingly, tap into the car's built-in microphone and listen to conversations happening in the car. The article clearly has a hard national security angle, but it raises a good point about the lack of mandatory security standards for internet connected cars - "road vehicles" are actually exempt from the Cyber Security (Security Standards for Smart Devices) Rules that took effect in March for things like baby monitors and smart watches!

Android users can create a Signal account without a phone number

Signal is now letting Android users sign up for an account without a phone number. The process is pretty straightforward: when you go to register a new Signal account, select "Register Without Phone Number" and you'll be asked for a once off payment of US$2.99/€3.49 via Google Pay. Signal says this is required to "help prevent spam" and that "this in-app purchase is never directly associated with the new Signal account". Instead of a username and password, you get a 32-digit Account ID and 64-digit Recovery Key that's used to log in. Lose those details and you're cooked, Signal does not provide a way to restore access to your account if you misplace them! Kinda crap you gotta pay, but I assume "prevent spam" means stopping what happened to Telegram, where I used to get daily messages from randos trying to start pig butchering scams.

Something I Saw On The Internet

Third Slip is a nice iOS cricket score app with Live Activities

Heads up cricket fans, Zach Simone has a new app called Third Slip (great name) that "uses Live Activities on your iPhone to surface the score everywhere it matters: on your Lock Screen, in the Dynamic Island, on your Apple Watch, and even in your car through CarPlay". You can also just open the app and see the scores, but the real focus here is what I would say ambient following of cricket match status. I'm a fan of Zach's other apps too - Petty, for petrol price searching and Glucomate, for everything you could ever want related to blood glucose tracking. I don't use them because my car doesn't take petrol and GLP-1 medication keeps my blood glucose immaculate, but I like that there's still indie developers out there making good quality iOS apps and want to encourage more of it.
